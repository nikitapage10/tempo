# TEMPO — Product overview

*This document describes TEMPO as it exists today. It is updated whenever the product changes.*

## What TEMPO is

TEMPO is a web app for managing a musician’s work from idea through release and beyond — not only production stages, but also the pitching, social posts, edit packs, and follow-ups that surround a track. It is a dark, studio-feeling workspace you open in a browser (phone or desktop), with your account and data living in the cloud so nothing depends on one machine. You can install it as an app on your phone or computer (PWA) with the same dark look.

## Who it’s for

A working artist who wants one place for tracks, projects, and tasks instead of a spreadsheet, notes app, and scattered files. You sign in with your email and manage your catalog. You can also invite collaborators onto a **single track** (mixer, vocalist, friend) with limited roles — TEMPO is not a full team workspace.

## The big ideas

Three kinds of things sit at the center of TEMPO: **tracks** (a musical work with stages, versions, and notes), **projects** (a work container you define — a campaign, a release push, a pack of related work, whatever you need — with an optional release workspace when the type calls for it), and **tasks** (actionable items that may or may not link to a track or a project). A project is not “one track,” and a track does not need a project: you can attach several tracks and tasks to one project, leave a project with none, or leave tracks free-standing. Splitting the Tracks list into albums, EPs, or playlists is what **groups** on Tracks are for — separate from projects. Progress is meant to feel honest — stage and “momentum” matter as much as checklist percentages — and each track keeps pinned milestone bounces plus the latest two unpinned ones so history that matters stays while storage stays lean.

## Current feature set

**Origin presentation.** Origin's spoken beats clear before the next panel
arrives, and its optional prompts melt fluidly from one suggestion into
another. The opening invitation shares the first film's poster and colour
grade. Identity and backstory use editorial glass compositions instead of
conventional form cards. Its signal narrative asks the artist to name the work,
give it a history through the moments and influences that shaped it, and show
where it is pointing now. The product name stays out of the story until the
final workspace threshold. A quiet ambient track begins only after **Tune in**
and loops through the full experience. After the artist gives the signal a
history, one grounded question asks what they are making now and what they want
it to feel like. The scrolling film is the only reflection and review, with
editable chapters for About, The sound, Right now, and The story. The first chapter uses an
asymmetrical lit composition, and the final transition holds its true last
frame until the scroll film is painted. The dashboard stays covered until the
closing arrival is ready to crossfade. Opening **Bring your music in** settles
the complete Import chapter at full clarity immediately; longer Import content
scrolls inside the chapter while the outer story stays locked.

Every later story chapter carries its own numbered light rail, ambient geometry
and content-specific composition rather than falling back to a plain text box.
The chapter-opening film begins dissolving into its exported final still before
it ends; the scroll film then rises over that still slowly, so neither decoder
timing nor an ended-video rewind can flash through. Entering the workspace uses
a persistent horizon-and-aperture reveal that begins before navigation and
holds until the dashboard is ready beneath it.

**Messaging inbox.** Notifications, Messages, and Search sit together in the
global top-right toolbar. The Messages icon carries an unread badge and opens a
mini-inbox for recent artist and TEMPO Support conversations, including quick
reply and a path to the full Messages page. The full inbox separates active and
archived conversations; a new reply returns an archived thread to the
recipient's inbox. Senders can remove their own messages, dictate into the
composer, or attach up to four private images, audio files, documents, or ZIPs.
Selecting a thread in the mini-inbox replaces the list with a focused quick
reply view for that conversation. A **New message** action in both the
mini-inbox and the Messages page finds any artist on the network by name or
handle and opens a conversation with them, so a first message no longer has to
start from someone's profile page; artists with DMs closed are declined with a
plain explanation. Incoming direct and support replies update
the mini-inbox, full thread, unread badges, and notification center in realtime,
with a timed reconnect fallback. The same composer and archive behavior applies
to member/admin support replies.

**Admin and support experience.** The Admin overview is an operating dashboard:
it calls out support and moderation work needing attention, shows 30-day member
activation and growth, and links directly into the relevant queues. Usage
analytics switches between 7-, 30-, and 90-day windows, with line/area charts
and explicit empty states for assistant use, storage growth, uploads, and focus
time. These remain aggregate measurements and never expose creative content.
Live count badges beside Support and Reports keep open queue work visible from
every Admin screen.

Every member support report becomes a private TEMPO Support conversation inside
Messages. An admin can reply under the support identity, the member can answer
in the same thread, and a member reply reopens a resolved ticket. This channel
is separate from artist direct messages and works for members who stay off the
social network. Admin Invites also shows whether server delivery settings are
present, which sender/domain is in use, and a safe actionable Resend rejection
instead of a generic provider error.

**Calendar.** Calendar is its own desktop and mobile planning workspace. It gathers task due dates, track targets and next moves, project deadlines, releases, and pitching deadlines without making duplicate records. Month, Agenda, and a six-stage creative Timeline cover day-to-day scheduling and the path from writing through release. You can drag or bulk-move authoritative dates, place unscheduled work using workload-aware suggestions, search, save filter presets, generate a release plan, spot workload or participant conflicts, and create events using natural language. Standalone events support recurrence, in-app reminders, milestones and dependencies, participants, comments and activity, links and briefs, duplication, and completion. Calendar exports to CSV or print-to-PDF but deliberately does not synchronize with external calendars. Event pills and Agenda rows use the same cursor-following edge light as TEMPO's tracks, tasks, and projects.

Today uses a simple sun in navigation while Calendar keeps its calendar glyph, so the two destinations stay distinct.

**Sign-in.** You open the live site and land on a two-column sign-in screen: the TEMPO wordmark and form on the left (a plain, solid panel), a large rounded window showing the animated Spectra light field on the right. You can sign in with email and password, or tap “Continue with Google” or “Continue with Microsoft” (each provider has to be enabled in Supabase before it works). There’s a Forgot password link that emails a reset link so you can set a new one. The password field has a show/hide toggle. Sign-in and create-account link to Terms and Privacy. The sign-in screen itself is plain — no intro plays before you're in. You can sign out from Settings. Settings is organized into Studio, Catalog, Notifications, and Account tabs — each tab shows only its own settings. **Notifications** is the full activity inbox (with filters); the bell in the header still keeps recent ones close. **Account** covers email and password, sign-out, links to profile visibility and who can message you, help, and deleting the account. **Your data** lets you export your catalog metadata as a file, restore from a file (including older or messy JSON reshaped with the same AI as Import), save a snapshot now, and merge missing rows back from automatic snapshots TEMPO keeps for you. Platform admins can see that backups exist, but not open the contents — export and restore stay with you. Audio bounces are not part of those backups — keep those in your own archives.

**Boot moment.** Once a day, the first time you enter the app after signing in, a short video plays as the loading screen while your workspace loads behind it: a spark igniting into the app's ice/white/amber light, with the TEMPO wordmark revealing letter by letter over it. At the end the picture disperses into prismatic streaks and dissolves through to the app. A **Skip** button appears after a moment, and clicking anywhere or pressing any key jumps straight past it. It's skipped entirely if you prefer reduced motion.

**Creating an account.** Create account is one link away from sign-in and uses the same layout, but making a new account needs an invite code — anyone who doesn't have one sees a "Request an invite" link that emails you directly. The provider sign-in buttons aren't offered on this screen, since only the coded form can be gated.

**App shell.** Once signed in you land on **Today** — unless you've just created your account, in which case TEMPO opens **Origin** first (see below). Desktop has a left rail and a short animated light strip along the top (wide enough to see the Spectra motion); phone uses a bottom tab bar. What's in the rail depends on the active space's focus (see Spaces below): a music-focus space shows Today, Board, Tracks, Projects, Tasks, Artist, Social and Stats, plus Settings (phone: Today, Board, Tasks, plus Add); a tasks-focus space drops Board and Tracks and shows Today, Projects, Tasks and Artist, plus Settings (phone: the same three plus Add). Artist, Social and Stats are always there, since they roll up every space rather than belonging to one. Under Settings in the rail, the current app version is shown in small mono type. A glowing **search** bar sits at the top-right of every main screen, lined up with the page edge: a slow ice–amber light rolls around the border, brightening into a soft halo and spinning faster while you hover or type. Type and results appear grouped by tracks, projects, tasks, people, messages, feed posts (yours and who you follow), board notes, stages, spaces, and pages. It looks across the active artist's whole catalog — titles, artist names, BPM, keys, tags, genres, next moves, and more — with a filter to narrow the category, keyboard arrows to pick a result, and ⌘K / Ctrl+K to jump to the bar. Messages covers artist conversations and TEMPO Support tickets, active and archived, searching who a thread is with and what was actually said in it; opening an archived result switches the Messages page to Archived. Search forgives spelling: a typo or one wrong word still finds the right thing, and when the closest match is a real stretch it says so and offers it as a "did you mean" instead. Opening a track from another space switches you into that space. A floating **assistant** sits in the bottom-right on every main screen (not on Focus or Import): open it to ask how TEMPO works or what's overdue in your catalog. You can type, talk (live dictation, or record-and-transcribe where live isn't available), or attach a screenshot, PDF, or text file with your question. When you clearly want something done — edit a track (BPM, key, genre, title, type, stage, momentum, deadline, next move, blocked/waiting), add a task, mark one done, set a due date, add a track or project, or open a screen — it proposes the change and you confirm with one tap before anything is written. It won't delete anything.

**Today.** A greeting banner shows the date, and — when you've uploaded one — your artist logo large in the bottom-right. In a **music-focus** space it also shows three quick counts (active tracks, tasks due this week, sessions this week) and a plain-English line on how much focus time you've logged this week; below that, **Tasks due** (checkable, overdue in warm red) and **Needs attention** — active tracks ordered by transparent reasons (blocked, waiting, overdue next move, quiet too long, and so on), each with a short explanation and a one-tap action like Open or Set next move; quick actions are + Track, + Task, Log session, Start focus; and a **cover art strip** at the bottom drifts your tracks in two slow opposite rows — tap any cover to open that track. Tracks without uploaded art get a temporary Spectra cover shaped by the track's title — vertical light slits from a design language (ice / white / amber / gray), over a soft atmospheric background with a broad hush of colors so covers are easier to tell apart. That same placeholder shows anywhere artwork would (Today, Tracks, Board, track header, projects). In a **tasks-focus** space, Today instead shows Due / Open / Projects counts, quick actions are just + Task and + Project, and the lower section lists your open tasks alongside project progress — no tracks, sessions, or cover strip, since the space doesn't use them. If a focus session is running anywhere, a banner up top lets you jump back in (or close it out if it's been running unusually long). An empty Today uses a contained light panel with a short invite — to bring your existing catalog in for a music space, or to add a task for a tasks-focus space.

**Origin — the first time you arrive.** A brand-new artist doesn't land in an empty workspace. Origin opens on a faint signal that the artist tunes into, names, and gives a history through the moments, influences, and instincts that shaped the work. It then asks what they are making right now and what they want it to feel like. A quiet ambient track starts with the **Tune in** gesture and loops through the experience. The artist can speak or type; both paths are equal and every answer stays theirs to edit. There is no separate recap screen. The scrolling reveal is the single place where the signal reflects the artist's words back as **About the artist**, **The sound**, **Right now**, and a modular **The story**, followed by **Bring the work into range** and **Keep tuning**. Every chapter has a small Back control, and going backward preserves the draft. The reading never declares a destiny, looks the artist up, invents releases or collaborators, or guesses at anything personal. About identifies who the artist is and what music they make; The sound describes audible qualities rather than repeating genres or location; Right now stays in the present; and story chapters carry the history without duplicating the bio. Every part is editable, and story chapters can be added, removed, and reordered. Bringing existing music in remains inside the film, and nothing reaches the catalog until the artist approves the final plan. Nothing is published or shared automatically. Approved details fill only still-empty profile fields and never change profile visibility. The name TEMPO appears only at the final threshold into the workspace, where the soundtrack fades with the closing film. Existing artists are never forced into Origin; it remains available under Settings → Artist Origin to revisit or replay, and it can be skipped without losing what was already entered.

**Bringing your music in.** You don't have to type your catalog in by hand. For a new account, **Bring your music in** is a set of chapters inside Origin; it also stays available as its own screen afterwards under Settings → Import (and from a link in the New track box, for when adding them one at a time isn't the right tool). It works as a conversation: type in the message box, hit the mic and talk (your words appear live as you speak, so you can fix a misheard title before sending), or drop files straight into the thread — screenshots of project folders, a notes app, a whiteboard, a distributor page, or a spreadsheet, PDF, Word file, or plain text. Drop a TEMPO catalog export from Settings and it offers a direct merge restore instead of guessing from scratch. Everything else goes into one thread; you don't have to choose the "right" format first. TEMPO reads each thing as it arrives and answers — it says what it actually found ("that screenshot lists 12 folders; looks like 8 songs") and asks about what's missing rather than waiting until the end to guess. You can answer in your own words or tap one of the suggested answers, and remove anything you'd rather not send. It states plainly that what you add is sent off to be read.

TEMPO then reads it all together and proposes a workspace: tracks with a type, stage and momentum, EPs and albums, tasks, target dates, BPMs and keys, next moves, and who you're waiting on. **Nothing is added to your catalog yet.** You get a review screen split into "Ready to add" and "Needs your eyes", where every value is editable and each one tells you where it came from and how confident TEMPO is — "From the folder 'EP Echoes'", "The spreadsheet lists this as out in March". Where something is genuinely ambiguous — three files that look like the same song at different stages, a title you already have, four tracks under one folder name that might be an EP — it asks you instead of guessing, with one-tap answers you can also type over, and redrafts using what you told it. Untick anything you don't want. A final screen shows exactly what will be created, and only then does "Build my TEMPO workspace" write anything — all at once, so a failure leaves you with nothing rather than half a catalog, and pressing it twice can't duplicate your catalog. Discarding an import deletes everything it held, including the files you uploaded, and those files are deleted once the workspace is built too. TEMPO won't search the internet for your discography, guess songwriting credits, or invent a release plan — it organises what you actually gave it. Song titles keep featuring credits exactly as they showed up (like "Midnight (feat. Lena)"); names it spots as collaborators are also kept as a note on the track, but inviting people properly still happens from the track itself. Audio bounces aren't part of this yet — this brings in the shape of your catalog, and you upload bounces to tracks as usual.

**Artists.** One account can hold more than one artist name. Every space, and so every track, project and task, belongs to an artist; you switch between them from the left rail, and manage them (add, rename, reorder, delete) in Settings. An existing catalog is moved onto a single artist automatically, so if you only ever release under one name you never have to think about this. Deleting an artist is a hard delete and tells you first exactly how many spaces and tracks go with it; you always keep at least one.

Each artist carries its own look. You pick an accent palette from a fixed set of color options (including a black-and-white Noir), or open **Custom** for your own Cool / Warm colors, upload a **logo** (the wide mark that shows large on Today) and an optional **profile image** (either a portrait or an emblem), and set a banner — your own image, a deep color wash, or a two-color gradient via a Custom button. Profile images appear as circular avatars in the feed, messages, and around the Social globe; in profile headers they sit larger beside the artist name as a complete circle, blending into the banner through a broad artist-colour halo. A profile with no uploaded image leaves the header clean, while compact surfaces use a readable palette-coloured monogram set into a dark signal disc with subtle orbit accents. TEMPO's assistant keeps TEMPO's compact equalizer mark rather than borrowing the artist's identity. The palette follows that artist everywhere the app uses color to mean something: buttons and links, the board's stage-progress colours, the placeholder covers TEMPO draws for tracks with no artwork (so a catalog of un-arted tracks reads as one set), the waveform player, and the moving light field that shows through Today and the edge strips. A bounce shared through a guest review link shows that artist's colours to whoever opens it. The banner appears behind the greeting on Today and on the artist's card in Settings; the logo shows bottom-right on Today (and in that Settings banner preview).

The customization is deliberately bounded so TEMPO still looks like TEMPO for every artist: the palettes are curated pairs rather than a free colour picker, backgrounds and text stay fixed, green still means done and red still means blocked no matter whose workspace you're in, and the TEMPO wordmark in the rail is never replaced by an artist's logo. An artist with nothing customized looks exactly like TEMPO always has.

**Spaces.** On first sign-in TEMPO creates two workspaces for your first artist: **Originals** and **Edits & Remixes**, each with a default stage pipeline. A new artist gets the same two to start. Switch spaces from the rail; manage them (rename, reorder, delete, and set focus) in Settings, where the list shows the spaces belonging to the artist you currently have selected. First sign-in also seeds four checklist templates.

Every space has a **focus**, set when you create it and changeable anytime in Settings: **Music** (the default — Board and a stage pipeline for tracks) or **Tasks & projects** (no board — for non-music work like running social media, where Tasks and Projects are the whole point). Switching a space's focus doesn't move or delete anything; it only changes what the rail and Today show for that space. Tasks and projects belong to whichever space was active when you created them, and only show up in that space.

**Artist profile.** **Artist** in the rail is a public-ready portrait of the work rather than a stack of biography fields. The banner, profile image, logo, name and tagline lead, followed by up to six deliberately featured music links. **About** explains who the artist is and what music they make, with genres and roles integrated into the same section. **Right now** shows the release, direction, or question currently carrying energy. **The sound** holds up to five audible qualities, contrasts, or ideas in the work. **Listen and connect** gives outside links their own compact home. **The story** is a set of artist-owned chapters displayed openly on the page; each chapter can be added, edited, removed, and reordered from Origin or the profile editor. Origin can provide the first draft of each public-facing section while every part remains editable. Private creative guidance remains in Origin and is not shown on the public profile. The editor also holds location, pronouns, a handle, up to 12 general links, and who can message you. Location suggests cities as you type and places the artist on the Social globe. Sharing stays compact in the header: profiles remain **Private** by default, or can be made visible to **TEMPO members** or at a **Public** link. A Preview action shows what others will see. The handle is the short lowercase @name used for that address and @mentions; the artist name remains prominent everywhere else. Signed-in profiles retain Follow and Message actions.

**Social.** **Social** in the rail is your network layer, and everything on it unlocks once you're on the network — Social (and Messages) offer a one-tap join, or you can change it on Artist. A slowly spinning **globe** sits under the tabs at all times — the upper half cresting up from the page and fading into the background below — pinning you and the people you're connected to where you actually are in the world, drawn from the location on each artist's profile (anyone who hasn't set one simply isn't plotted). Scroll to zoom in; when several people sit close together (a busy Europe, for example), only some faces show until you zoom closer and they have room to separate. There's no hard ring around the planet; just a soft atmosphere past the edge. Hovering a face eases the spin down and shows their name, how you know them, and where they are; clicking opens their profile. You can drag the globe to spin it yourself. It's painted in your own artist palette, with the color washing slowly across the continents. Three tabs sit above it. **Top 8** is your own pick of up to eight people pinned for quick access — chosen from anyone you follow or any contact linked to a TEMPO profile — each one clicking through to their page. **Follows** shows who you follow and who follows you. **Discover** searches published artist profiles by name, and before you type anything it lists artists you've recently worked with, so it's a place to reconnect rather than an empty search box. Down the right-hand side, a **feed** column lets you post updates (optional image, optional attach one of your own tracks), @mention handles, like and comment; the composer starts as a single line and opens up when you click into it, visibility is followers-only, TEMPO members, or public, and your home feed is your posts plus posts from people you follow. A post can be reported from the feed, and another artist can be reported from their published profile; the operator receives only that specific public item. **Messages** (from Social or its own screen) is direct messaging between artist profiles, with unread counts that clear when you open the thread.

**Stats.** Today, Board, Tracks and Tasks all show one space at a time; **Stats** (formerly "Artist", renamed and moved to its own tab when the Artist tab became the public profile page above) shows the whole artist's numbers at once, across every space they own. Reach it from the rail or the artist dropdown. It opens on that artist's banner and logo with a headline row — tracks, bounces, in progress, released, focus time — and then a set of sections: **the year in bounces** (twelve months of bounces uploaded against tracks started, so a year of work reads at a glance); **pipeline** (where tracks sit in each space's stages, one bar per space, with spaces that have nothing staged collected into a single quiet line); **spaces** (every space of that artist side by side with its own counts and when it was last touched — tap one to switch to it); **your sound** (the shape of your tempos, the keys you write in most, your genres and track types); **work rhythm** (when you actually work, by day and hour, with your busiest day, busiest hour and how many weeks in a row you've kept going); **longest in progress** (the oldest unfinished tracks and how long each has sat in its current stage); **releases** (countdowns to what's coming and a strip of what's already out); and **feedback received** (how much has come from guests versus your own notes, how much is still open, and the decisions logged). Every number comes from what's actually in your TEMPO — nothing is estimated or guessed.

**Streaming platforms.** The Stats page can also show how your music is doing outside TEMPO. Paste your profile link once per platform. **SoundCloud** is the one that gives real numbers — plays, followers, likes and reposts, plus your most-played tracks. **Spotify** and **Apple Music** list your releases as each service has them, and nothing more: Spotify removed follower counts, popularity and top tracks from its API in February 2026, and Apple's statistics need a paid developer membership. Every section states plainly what it cannot show. No platform has ever published real stream counts or monthly listeners to outside apps — Spotify's own live only in Spotify for Artists — and TEMPO never estimates or fills in the gaps.

Because SoundCloud only ever hands over *today's* totals and no history, TEMPO writes the numbers down once a day and draws the trend itself. A freshly linked section shows a flat figure on the first day and a line from the second onwards, so the curve builds the longer you use it. Linking is per artist, so each artist name you release under points at its own profiles. You never need a developer account of your own: the platform keys belong to TEMPO and are shared by everyone using it, so an artist's whole setup is pasting a link.

The page is **modular**, the same way a track workspace is. "Edit layout" in the top-right of the banner lets you drag sections between the two columns, reorder them, drop one onto another to combine them into a tabbed panel, and hide anything you don't want — hidden sections stay one click away in a tray, and Reset puts everything back. From the same panel you can start from a template — **Overview** (everything), **Minimal**, **Statistics**, or **Platforms** — each a different focused starting arrangement you can still rearrange freely afterwards. Your arrangement is remembered per artist, on your account — the same however you sign in. The charts use that artist's own Cool / Warm colours, adjusted so large filled shapes stay readable against the dark background, and stage colours match the Board's cold-to-hot ramp so the two screens agree.

**Custom stat modules.** Anything worth tracking that TEMPO doesn't already show — sync placements, merch sold, radio spins, an Instagram follower count, whatever — gets its own module you build yourself. From "Edit layout", add a new custom module and give it a name; it lands on the page like any other section. Inside it, add as many named stats as you want (an optional unit like "$" or "plays"), and log a reading for any date right from the card — no need to re-open Edit layout. Log as often as you actually update the number; each reading is kept, so a stat with two or more readings draws its own filled area chart with a date underneath each end. Made a mistake? Each stat shows its last few readings with a one-tap undo. Rename or remove a whole module from its own header. These numbers are the one thing on the Artist page TEMPO doesn't calculate itself — you're the source of truth for them.

**Board.** Kanban for the active space: drag tracks and sticky notes between stages, Sort and Filter from compact header menus (type, tag, attention; order cards in a column by Custom, title, updated, or deadline), choose Comfortable or Compact card density, tap a track card to open the workspace. Each stage has a **+** to place an existing track that has no stage, start a new track there, or add a sticky note (title + optional text — board-only, not in Tracks). Tracks with no stage stay hidden on the Board until you add them from that **+** menu (they’re still in Tracks). A muted **Remove** on the card clears the stage without deleting the track. Comfortable cards show cover, type, and next move; Compact is a thin text row (title plus a tiny momentum mark) so dense boards stay scannable. The whole pipeline stays on screen without scrolling sideways — stages share the available width, and a stage holding nothing shrinks to a slim labelled strip until you start dragging, at which point every stage opens up so you can drop anywhere. On a phone or a narrow window the stages stack vertically instead. Cards show a small mark when something’s blocked or overdue.

**Tracks.** Create with title and type (more details optional). The Tracks page lists everything in the active space — Sort and Filter open from compact header menus (Custom, title, stage, updated, deadline, plus any saved orders; type, stage, tag, attention), and Comfortable / Compact density matches the Board (Compact is a thin text row). Custom is the default: drag the handle to rearrange. You can also split the list into **groups** (an album, EP, playlist, or any bucket you name) — separate from Projects. Create a group from the header, drag tracks into it, or remove a group without deleting its tracks (they simply stop being in one). Each group can carry an optional cover image (shown as a thumbnail beside the name) and an optional color that tints the whole block — one of five fixed swatches or any custom color you pick — so an EP reads as one thing rather than another anonymous list; a group with neither looks plain. Reorder the groups themselves by dragging the handle beside the name, or with the arrows. Tracks that aren't in any group get no heading at all and sit at the top of the list by default; you can move a group above them if you'd rather. Group covers and colors need migration 044; a custom (rather than preset) color needs migration 045. **Save** names the current arrangement and adds it to Sort so you can return to it later (apply it as Custom, overwrite it, or remove it). Hit "Select" to tick several tracks and delete them in one go, with a confirmation naming each one.

**Track workspace.** A studio-feeling page built around listening. The header shows artwork (or a gradient placeholder, tap to set cover) tinted with a soft wash of the artwork's own color, editable title, mono meta line, momentum, and deadline. Below it, a stage timeline shows every stage as a row you click through (or move between with arrow keys) — jumping more than one stage at once asks you to confirm, and a small lightning-bolt mark shows which stages have an automation ("recipe") set up. If a stage's recipe is in "preview" mode, moving into it opens a dialog listing what it would do so you can pick which parts to apply; "automatic" mode runs everything right away with a toast you can undo. The main column is the waveform player (current or selected version, A/B via dropdown, with small markers on the waveform wherever a comment is pinned and an "Add comment here" button next to play/pause), a version timeline (upload with “what changed?”, set current, download, delete, pin as a milestone, record a decision — see below — pinned milestones are kept plus the two newest unpinned bounces; wav/aiff are converted to mp3 in the browser before upload; deleting a version that's pinned, has comments/decisions, or is on an active guest link tells you so before you confirm), a guest review links section for sharing a bounce outside TEMPO, a Now / Next / Blocked / Target strip for tracking your next move, who you're waiting on, any blocker, and your target date (with a short list of plain-English attention flags underneath, including how many comments are still open), a workflow strip for starting/continuing a focus session and seeing recent stage automations (with a retry for anything that failed), and the session log. **The whole page is modular.** Every section — player, versions, workflow strip, guest links, session log, checklist, comments, files, notes, references, people, activity and details (metadata plus Delete track) — is a module you can arrange. "Edit layout" lets you drag modules between the left and right columns, reorder them, and hide what you don't need; dropping one module onto another combines the two into a tabbed panel so related tools share a single space. A divider between the columns can be dragged (or nudged with arrow keys) to set how wide each side is. Presets — Writing, Production, Feedback, Mix review, Release prep — each lay out a focused arrangement for that kind of session rather than showing everything at once, and anything a preset leaves out stays one click away in a "Hidden" tray. The waveform can't be hidden while a track has bounces. Layouts save per track and are personal to you — collaborators keep their own. An **Edit** menu in the bottom-right of the track's header holds "Edit layout" and "Delete track", so both stay reachable whatever modules your layout is showing. Deleting asks you to confirm and tells you its bounces, comments, checklists and session history go with it. Uploading artwork (from the header or Files tab) sets the track cover. Audio lives in private cloud storage and plays via time-limited links; large bounces need your Storage size limit raised in Supabase if uploads over ~50 MB fail.

**Milestones, decisions & blind A/B.** Pin any version as a milestone (rough mix, final master, etc. — with an optional label) so it stands out in the version timeline; unpinning warns you first if that bounce would then become eligible for automatic cleanup. Record a decision on a version — approved, needs changes, or rejected, with an area (mix, master, arrangement, artwork, general) and a note — decisions pile up as an append-only log, with the latest shown right on the version row. To compare two versions blind, select them in the timeline and start a Blind A/B: which one is "A" and which is "B" is shuffled and hidden, you play one at a time at your own volume for each, and only once you reveal do you see which was which — at that point you can log your pick and optionally record it as a decision.

**Stage recipes.** Any stage can have a recipe — one or more automations that fire when a track moves into it: apply a checklist template, create a task, set the track's next move, set momentum, or ask for a decision on the current version. Recipes run in "preview" mode by default (you get to pick which actions to apply each time) or can be switched to "automatic" (they run immediately, with a toast offering to undo the stage change). Writing, Production, Mixdown, Master, and Release Prep stages offer a one-click "add suggested recipe" starting point. This applies everywhere a track's stage changes — dragging on the Board, clicking through the track's own timeline, or using the stage dropdown.

**Focus sessions.** Start a focus session from a track's page or from Today — give it a goal and, optionally, pull in a few checklist items to work from. That drops you into a stripped-down session screen: the track's identity, your goal, a live elapsed timer, the waveform, your chosen checklist items, and private scratch notes (not saved anywhere else — leaving with unsaved notes prompts a confirm). Ending a session asks what changed, what's left, and your next move (with the option to set that as the track's official next move), lets you check off any of the session's checklist items, and lets you bounce up a new version right there. Abandoning a session discards the timer without logging it as complete. Sessions still running after 24 hours get a "close or keep going" prompt instead of a plain continue. The track's session log shows goal, duration, and outcome for these alongside older, simpler logged sessions.

**References.** A References tab on the track page holds anything that informs the track without being part of it: another track or sound (as an audio clip, optionally with a start/end trim), an image, a link, or a plain note — each with an optional "why this matters" line, reorderable by drag. Audio references play through the same one-at-a-time player as everything else, so they never overlap the main waveform. Star a reference to carry it into your next focus session, where it shows up in a small dedicated section.

**Comments.** Pin feedback to the exact moment in a bounce: click "Add comment here" while listening (or a marker on the waveform) to jump straight to the Comments tab with that timestamp ready to go, or write a general note with no timestamp. Comments can be replied to, edited, resolved (and reopened), or deleted with confirm. Switch between "this version" and "all versions" — clicking a comment from another version loads that bounce and seeks to the right spot automatically. The Comments tab shows how many threads are still open, and that count also shows up in the track's attention flags.

**Guest review links.** Share one specific bounce with someone who doesn't have (or need) a TEMPO account — a mixer, a vocalist, a friend whose ears you trust. From the track page's guest links section, pick a version, an optional label, an expiry (14 days by default, or 7/30/never), and whether they can comment and/or download; TEMPO shows the shareable link exactly once, so copy it before closing. The guest opens a plain, focused page — track title, artwork, that version's waveform, and (if you allowed it) a simple name-and-note comment box, optionally pinned to a moment — with no sign-in, no ability to see your notes, checklist, other versions, or edit/resolve anything, and the page is kept out of search engines. Revoke a link anytime from the same panel without deleting comments the guest already left; expired or revoked links show a calm "not available" page instead of any error that reveals why.

**Tasks layout.** The task list is laid out as four columns — Overdue, Today, This week, Later — so the shape of your week is visible at a glance; completed tasks collect below. Filters sit in one compact toolbar with a Clear link once anything is set.

**Tasks.** List scoped to the active space, with quick-add, category chips, status, due date, optional link to a track or project, and notes. Grouped into Overdue / Today / This week / Later; filters by category and status. Linked items show as chips that jump to the track or project.

**Projects.** Card grid scoped to the active space, showing name, type, deadline, track/task counts, and combined checklist %. A project is whatever you call one — create it as General, Single, EP, Album, or Edit pack if that label helps, then attach or detach any number of existing tracks and tasks (or none). It is not a one-track wrapper and not the same thing as a Tracks-page group. Open a project to edit description and deadline. Release-type projects unlock a **release workspace**: release date and countdown, a transparent readiness list (what’s still missing — not a mystery percentage), track order and per-track metadata/credits with copy/CSV export, distribution and pitching fields, a timeline of dates and tasks, an optional “create release plan” preview of suggested tasks, master/artwork warnings when a milestone doesn’t match the current bounce, and a post-release section once a live link and past date exist.

**People & collaboration.** On a track’s People tab, the owner can invite someone by email with a role (editor, uploader, commenter, or viewer), copy a one-time invite link (no email send yet — you share the link yourself), change roles, or revoke access. The invitee signs in with the matching email and accepts. Collaborators only see that track — not your whole catalog, projects list, or unrelated tasks. Controls they can’t use are hidden or disabled; the database still enforces the rules.

**Activity & notifications.** Each track has an Activity tab with a readable history of meaningful events (uploads, stage moves, comments, and similar — not every keystroke). The app shell has a notification center with an unread count; you’ll get in-app notices for things like invite acceptance and new versions on shared tracks. Program invitations can be delivered by email; there are no general email notifications yet.

**Help & reporting.** **Report a problem** beside Settings opens a short bug, help, or feedback form; the Settings page carries the same control for mobile. It sends only what you deliberately type, a privacy-safe label for the current page, and basic browser information. You can also describe a bug or help request to the TEMPO assistant: it prepares the support report and asks you to confirm before sending it.

**Workspace customize.** On a track page, Customize lets you pick a layout preset (Writing, Production, Feedback, Mix review, Release prep, or Custom), reorder or hide optional modules, choose the default work-panel tab, and toggle compact mode. Layouts are yours alone — they don’t change what collaborators see. The waveform stays available whenever a bounce exists.

**Checklists & templates.** Arrangement, Mixdown, Master Prep, and Release Prep ship on first login. Apply a template to a track or save any checklist as a new template.

**Origin motion and sound.** TEMPO’s voice melts from one thought into the next
throughout Origin, not just in its opening lines. Copy and interactive panels
take separate turns rather than overlapping, and each film soundtrack eases
fully to silence before its scene is released.

**Look & feel.** Dark Spectra palette (ice for interaction, amber for “current” / status). Two typefaces: a distinct display face for titles and the logo, and one workhorse face for everything else, including all numbers — BPM, keys, timestamps, counts. Anything you can click that represents a track, project, or task — board cards, list rows, the attention queue on Today — glows softly around the cursor in the app's own ice → white → amber colours (amber-warning automatically if it's blocked or overdue). The logo itself is built from that same light rather than a static image. Surfaces are layered and lit — panels sit on the background with a soft top edge and a cast shadow, so what matters on a screen looks like it matters, rather than every section being an identical outlined box. Today opens on a hero carrying an oversized greeting, your active / due / session counts as large readable numerals, and the quick actions. Prismatic light appears as intro, top edge, empty states, and behind the Today hero, where the cover over it fades out toward the edges so the motion is actually visible — never as a full-page background behind dense data. Keyboard focus uses a clear ice ring. Errors surface as short toasts with what to try next.

**Program administration.** TEMPO has a private, unlisted Admin console for running the invited program. A platform administrator can see account identity, join and sign-in dates, public profile identity, and aggregate usage analytics—including 90-day assistant-request, storage-growth, upload, and focus-time trends. Admins can issue or revoke one-person signup links; send a branded email containing an individual code and one-click signup link; see delivery status and resend; triage member-submitted bugs, help requests, and product feedback; suspend, reactivate, or permanently remove an account; review reports that point to content a member published to the network; and inspect an audit trail of every privileged change. The console cannot fetch members’ track or project names, audio, lyrics, notes, checklists, feedback, ordinary comments, session contents, direct messages, private profiles, or private contact books.

## How it’s hosted

The live app is at **https://tempo-ten-sigma.vercel.app** (sign-in at `/login`). Pushes to the main branch deploy there automatically. For local work on a Mac, double-click **Launch TEMPO.command** in the project folder — it starts a local server and opens the app in your browser (keep that Terminal window open; Ctrl+C stops it). Local development uses your `.env.local` keys; anything that ships uses the production address for absolute links, not localhost.

The floating assistant’s product reference lives in the repo’s assistant knowledge file — update that when this document’s feature set changes so the assistant stays accurate.
