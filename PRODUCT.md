# TEMPO — Product overview

*This document describes TEMPO as it exists today. It is updated whenever the product changes.*

## What TEMPO is

TEMPO is a web app for managing a musician’s work from idea through release and beyond — not only production stages, but also the pitching, social posts, edit packs, and follow-ups that surround a track. It is a dark, studio-feeling workspace you open in a browser (phone or desktop), with your account and data living in the cloud so nothing depends on one machine. You can install it as an app on your phone or computer (PWA) with the same dark look.

## Who it’s for

A working artist who wants one place for tracks, projects, and tasks instead of a spreadsheet, notes app, and scattered files. You sign in with your email and manage your catalog. You can invite collaborators onto a **single track** (mixer, vocalist, friend) with limited roles, and separately bring on **team members** at the artist level — a manager, agent, tour manager, label contact, or assistant — with access scoped to exactly the areas you grant them (see Team below). Team members sign in as themselves: they are not turned into an artist. They get a hub of the artists they work with, their own projects and tasks, their own profile, and Social as themselves. One login can be both an artist and a manager — switch between your artist and your home. If someone is already on TEMPO as a team member and later receives an artist invite to the same email, they sign in with that account and go through Origin as an artist; they do not create a second login. After Origin they have all three: their own artist, their manager home, and the artists they work with.

## The big ideas

Three kinds of things sit at the center of TEMPO: **tracks** (a musical work with stages, versions, and notes), **projects** (a work container you define — a campaign, a release push, a pack of related work, whatever you need — with an optional release workspace when the type calls for it), and **tasks** (actionable items that may or may not link to a track or a project). A project is not “one track,” and a track does not need a project: you can attach several tracks and tasks to one project, leave a project with none, or leave tracks free-standing. Splitting the Tracks list into albums, EPs, or playlists is what **groups** on Tracks are for — separate from projects. Progress is meant to feel honest — stage and “momentum” matter as much as checklist percentages — and every bounce a track has ever had stays in its history; the cloud itself only ever holds the current bounce and the one before it (as mp3 — wav/aiff stay on TEMPO Desktop in their original format), moving older ones off the cloud (never deleting them) once a desktop copy is confirmed.

## Current feature set

**Onboarding motion.** The closing Origin and Passage story follows native trackpad and wheel movement while its film frames and glass panels are paced to remain fluid on Retina displays.

**Tracks catalog surface.** A populated Tracks list sits on a darker glass
surface over the shared moving wash. Group tints and cursor lighting remain
visible above it, but the catalog keeps the same grounded depth as Calendar.

**Origin presentation.** Origin's spoken beats clear before the next panel
arrives, and its optional prompts melt fluidly from one suggestion into
another. The opening invitation shares the first film's poster and colour
grade. Identity and backstory use editorial glass compositions instead of
conventional form cards. Its signal narrative asks the artist to name the work,
give it a history through the moments and influences that shaped it, and show
where it is pointing now. The product name stays out of the story until the
final workspace threshold. A quiet Tempo Theme bed begins only after **Tune in**
and loops through the full experience. After the artist gives the signal a
history, one grounded question asks what they are making now and what they want
it to feel like. Both questions use the same one-tap dictation control: tap to
start, tap again to stop, or pause briefly and let it stop on its own. Direction
uses a deeper glass surface so its text remains clear over bright film frames.
The scrolling film is the only reflection and review, with
editable chapters for About, The sound, Right now, and The story. The first chapter uses an
asymmetrical lit composition, and the final transition holds its true last
frame until the scroll film is painted. The dashboard stays covered until the
closing arrival is ready to crossfade. Opening **Bring your music in** settles
the complete Import chapter at full clarity immediately; longer Import content
scrolls inside the chapter while the outer story stays locked.

Every later story chapter carries its own numbered light rail, ambient geometry
and content-specific composition rather than falling back to a plain text box.
The chapter-opening film begins dissolving into its exported final still before
it ends; the scroll film then rises over that still slowly, so neither decoder
timing nor an ended-video rewind can flash through. Entering the workspace uses
a persistent horizon-and-aperture reveal that begins before navigation and

**Messaging inbox.** Notifications, Messages, your profile menu, and Search sit together in the
global top-right toolbar. It stays visually open at the top of a page, then fades in a dark frosted-glass backdrop as you scroll so artwork and content cannot compete with the controls. The profile button shows your artist photo and opens Artist profile, Stats,
Settings, a desktop handoff (Open web app on desktop; Open TEMPO or Download TEMPO on the web), Admin portal when you have program-admin access, and Sign out. Search understands tempo queries like `bpm 140` or
`140 through 150` so you get tracks in that speed range, not every note that
mentions BPM. The Messages icon carries an unread badge and opens a
mini-inbox for recent artist and TEMPO Support conversations, including quick
reply and a path to the full Messages page. The full inbox separates active and
archived conversations; a new reply returns an archived thread to the
recipient's inbox. Conversation names, unread counts, and message sides follow
the signed-in people in the thread, so switching between artist and Pro
workspaces cannot make one of your own artists appear to be the other sender.
The full conversation is a bounded, responsive workspace:
only its transcript scrolls, older history loads without moving your place, and
new replies do not pull you away while you are reading. Private realtime
delivery powers messages and short-lived typing indicators without exposing
read receipts, online status, or last seen information. Failed sends can be
retried, and an unsent draft is kept separately for every conversation.
Artists can reply to, react to, edit, delete, search, and pin direct messages;
deleted messages remain as placeholders and edits are marked. Scene chat uses
the same experience, with shared pins limited to Scene owners and moderators.
Support keeps replies, edits, search, voice, and media while omitting reactions
and pins. Inbox controls include mute, mark unread, archive, and shared media.
Message controls float beside a bubble without shifting the conversation away
from its edge, sent messages leave breathing room before the slim coloured
transcript scrollbar, and each side stays easy to scan.
The composer accepts pasted and dropped files with previews and upload progress,
up to four private files of 10 MB each.
Selecting a thread in the mini-inbox replaces the list with a focused quick
reply view for that conversation. A **New message** action in both the
mini-inbox and the Messages page finds any artist on the network by name or
handle and opens a conversation with them, so a first message no longer has to
start from someone's profile page; artists with DMs closed are declined with a
plain explanation. Incoming direct replies update the mini-inbox, full thread,
and Messages unread badge in realtime without creating a duplicate bell
notification. Away from Messages, a new message raises a glass toast in the bottom-right (above Get help on the web; the same glass card on desktop when the window is in the background), tinted to your active artist's Cool / Warm colours. On the Messages page itself the toast stays off — the conversation updates in place, and if a deep link is available TEMPO opens that thread instead of stacking another popup over the chat.
Support replies also update the notification center. Reconnects
reconcile missed changes, with polling retained only as a temporary fallback.
Dictation and voice notes are separate actions: dictated text remains editable,
while voice notes include duration, a waveform, playback progress, and playback
speed. The preferred input for the web app lives under Settings > Account;
TEMPO Desktop also keeps a quick microphone picker beneath the composer.
Recording uses the operating-system default unless a remembered input is
selected, falls back when that device disappears, and gives clear Windows or
macOS permission-recovery guidance when access is denied.

**Desktop updates.** TEMPO Desktop stays current without asking artists to
understand how an update is delivered. Once you’re on the public desktop

analytics switches between 7-, 30-, and 90-day windows, with line/area charts

**Calendar.** Calendar is its own desktop and mobile planning workspace, styled as frosted glass over the app's dark background rather than flat panels. It gathers task due dates, track targets and next moves, project deadlines, releases, and pitching deadlines without making duplicate records. Month, Week, Agenda, and a six-stage creative Timeline cover day-to-day scheduling and the path from writing through release. Week shows an hour-by-hour grid — drag an event to a new day or time, or click an empty hour to create one there — with all-day items (deadlines, releases, task due dates) kept in their own row above the hours. Calendar has an explicit display timezone (set from the calendar's "More" menu), so every timed item shows converted to one consistent zone with a small marker on anything scheduled in a different one; the event editor's timezone field is a searchable picker with the UTC offset shown, plus quick duration buttons. Right below the page title, a quick-add bar reads a plain sentence — typed or spoken, tap the microphone to dictate — and turns it into a real event or task ("Studio session Friday at 7pm", or "task: mix notes" to create a task instead); it asks TEMPO's assistant to understand the phrasing, and falls back to simple parsing instantly if that's ever unavailable. Clicking any day opens a detail panel with that day's full schedule, a **New event** button right under the date, a drop target for rescheduling, and an unscheduled-work section. The toolbar itself stays short — scope, saved filter views, categories, week-number and week-start settings, and CSV/print export live one click away in Filters and More menus rather than all being visible at once. You can drag or bulk-move authoritative dates, place unscheduled work using workload-aware suggestions, search, save and rename filter presets, generate a release plan, and spot workload, pitching-timing, or participant conflicts (surfaced as a single line only when something needs attention). Standalone events support recurrence, in-app reminders, milestones and dependencies, participants, comments and activity, links and briefs, duplication, and completion. Calendar exports to CSV or print-to-PDF but deliberately does not synchronize with external calendars. Event pills and Agenda rows use the same cursor-following edge light as TEMPO's tracks, tasks, and projects.

The signed-in workspace sits over one short, softly focused video loop, separate from the app-wide light-field. It stays mounted while you move between Today, Calendar, Board, Tracks, Projects, Tasks, Artist, Social, Scenes, Stats, and Settings, so the motion never restarts just because you changed pages. It backs the entire content area — everything but the left menu and bottom edge — and continues cleanly behind the global controls while each page is at the top. Once the page scrolls, a dark glass wash fades in behind those sticky controls for contrast, without bringing back the former bright strip across the top. Thin light lines under page titles and along the rail still open onto the Spectra field, so those rules keep their living texture while the video wash runs everywhere else. The subtly deeper dark wash stays fixed as each page scrolls and remains behind a legibility scrim. It pauses when the window is not in front, and degrades to a still poster frame under reduced motion or if autoplay is refused.

Today uses a simple sun in navigation while Calendar keeps its calendar glyph, so the two destinations stay distinct.

Starting a direct message opens the returned conversation immediately, including when that artist already has an archived thread with the sender.

**Sign-in.** You open the live site and land on a two-column sign-in screen: the TEMPO wordmark and form on the left (a plain, solid panel), a large rounded window showing the animated Spectra light field on the right. A quiet Tempo Theme bed fades in under the form (the same soundtrack Origin uses) — in the browser after your first click or key, and on TEMPO Desktop as soon as the track is ready. You can sign in with email and password, or tap “Continue with Google” or “Continue with Microsoft” (each provider has to be enabled in Supabase before it works). On TEMPO Desktop those provider sign-ins open in your normal browser (so Google/Microsoft don’t block the app as insecure), then hand you back into TEMPO signed in — even if the browser never offers to open a TEMPO link. Signing out and into another account starts that account clean — you won’t keep seeing the previous catalog, artist, or admin tools — and each account remembers its own real workspace. A Pro always wakes in their personal Home; an available demo remains something they deliberately open, not their default identity on a later login. After you are in, TEMPO Desktop quietly saves track and album covers, scene banners, and artist logos into its local vault in the background so the next open of those screens is faster; in the browser, TEMPO batch-signs and warms those same pictures so covers and logos fill in sooner (with Spectra or a tint holding the space while they arrive). There’s a Forgot password link that emails a reset link so you can set a new one. The password field has a show/hide toggle. Sign-in and create-account link to Terms and Privacy. Every member explicitly accepts the current policies before entering the private app; new members do it while creating an account, and existing or provider-sign-in members see a one-time agreement screen. The accepted version and time are recorded, and a material future update can ask members to accept again. The daily boot film still waits until you're signed in — it does not play on the sign-in screen itself. You can sign out from Settings. Settings is organized into Studio, Catalog, Notifications, and Account tabs — each tab shows only its own settings. **Notifications** opens on a **Pulse** section — email digest cadence (off, daily, or weekly), delivery time and timezone, which weekday a weekly digest lands on, which categories it covers, whether high-value moments (new guest feedback, a collaboration invite) can email immediately, and whether the email uses generic wording or actual track/project names (off by default) — followed by the full activity inbox (with filters); the bell in the header still keeps recent ones close. Pulse email is off until you turn it on, and every digest includes one-click links to manage preferences or unsubscribe. **Account** covers email and password, sign-out, links to profile visibility and who can message you, help, the Terms and Privacy policy, and deleting the account. The legal area states the central privacy promise in plain language: private music and workspace content is stored to run TEMPO, not routinely browsed in the admin portal, sold, used for advertising, or used to train AI models. A short **Product improvement data** note nearby explains the limited, anonymous usage events TEMPO records (things like "a track was created" or "a focus session finished") and states plainly what they never include — titles, filenames, lyrics, notes, messages, comments, or anything you wrote. **Your data** lets you export your catalog metadata as a file, restore from a file (including older or messy JSON reshaped with the same AI as Import), save a snapshot now, and merge missing rows back from automatic snapshots TEMPO keeps for you. Platform admins can see that backups exist, but not open the contents — export and restore stay with you. Audio bounces are not part of those backups — keep those in your own archives.

**Boot moment.** Once a day, the first time you enter the app after signing in, a short video plays as the loading screen while your workspace loads behind it: a spark igniting into the app's ice/white/amber light, with the TEMPO wordmark revealing letter by letter over it, and the quiet Tempo Theme bed continuing underneath. At the end the picture disperses into prismatic streaks and dissolves through to the app as the music fades. A **Skip** button appears after a moment, and clicking anywhere or pressing any key jumps straight past it. It's skipped entirely if you prefer reduced motion.

**Creating an account.** Create account is one link away from sign-in and uses the same layout, but making a new account needs an invite code — anyone who doesn't have one sees a "Request an invite" link that emails you directly. The exception is a **team or track invite**: if someone invited you to work with an artist (as a manager, for example) or onto a track, that link is enough — you create an account with your name, that email, and a password, with no separate invite code. Every new account asks for a display name so you show up as yourself on Team and Social instead of a placeholder. The form requires an explicit checkbox agreeing to the Terms and acknowledging the Privacy policy. The provider sign-in buttons aren't offered on this screen, since only the coded form can be gated.

**App shell.** Once signed in you land on **Today** — unless you've just created your account, in which case TEMPO opens a short **welcome chooser** (browser or Windows desktop download) and then **Origin** (see below). Desktop has a left rail and a short animated light strip along the top (wide enough to see the Spectra motion); phone uses a bottom tab bar. On a medium-width window the rail collapses to icons (artist and space show initials) so the controls stay clickable; labels return when the window is wide. What's in the rail depends on the active space's focus (see Spaces below): a music-focus space shows Today, Calendar, Board, Tracks, Projects, Tasks, Artist, and Social, plus Settings (phone: Today, Board, Tasks, plus Add). Hover **Artist** for Profile, Team, and Stats; hover **Social** for Network (the social page) and Scenes. Cutting diagonally toward Stats keeps the Artist menu; moving down the rail onto Social opens that menu right away. A tasks-focus space drops Board and Tracks and shows Today, Calendar, Projects, Tasks, Artist, and Social, plus Settings (phone: the same three plus Add). Artist, Team, Social, Scenes and Stats still roll up every space rather than belonging to one — they just live in those two hover menus so the rail stays short. A team member in their own home sees Today, Calendar, Board, Projects, Tasks, Profile, Artists, and Social (with Network and Scenes underneath) instead. Their Board is a task-status flow, not the artist's song Board or identity editor. Directly above Settings, the web app offers the desktop download until it recognizes a recent TEMPO Desktop installation, then changes to **Open in desktop** and launches the installed app on the same screen. In TEMPO Desktop, that same position says **Open web app** and opens the current page in your browser. Glass panels and heroes can carry a thin amber-to-ice side edge from Origin on the important shells — page heroes and Board columns — so Calendar and dense track grids stay clean. Board track cards keep the hover Spectra border instead of a permanent side line. Peer glass widgets share one soft drop shadow so a quieter tile next to a primary one does not look glued flat to the page. Under Settings in the rail, the current app version is shown in small mono type (on the full-width rail). The rail's media player keeps a queue of every track with a playable bounce in the active space, so Previous and Next continue working from Today or any other page and refresh when versions change. A glowing **search** bar sits at the top-right of every main screen, lined up with the page edge: a slow ice–amber light rolls around the border, brightening into a soft halo and spinning faster while you hover or type. Type and results appear grouped by tracks, projects, tasks, people, messages, feed posts (yours and who you follow), board notes, stages, spaces, and pages. It looks across the active artist's whole catalog — titles, artist names, BPM, keys, tags, genres, next moves, and more — with a filter to narrow the category, keyboard arrows to pick a result, and ⌘K / Ctrl+K to jump to the bar. Messages covers artist conversations and TEMPO Support tickets, active and archived, searching who a thread is with and what was actually said in it; opening an archived result switches the Messages page to Archived. Search forgives spelling: a typo or one wrong word still finds the right thing, and when the closest match is a real stretch it says so and offers it as a "did you mean" instead. Opening a track from another space switches you into that space. A floating **assistant** sits in the bottom-right on every main screen (not on Focus or Import): open it to ask how TEMPO works or what's overdue in your catalog. You can type, talk (live dictation, or record-and-transcribe where live isn't available), or attach a screenshot, PDF, or text file with your question. When you clearly want something done — edit a track (BPM, key, genre, title, type, stage, momentum, deadline, next move, blocked/waiting), add a task, mark one done, set a due date, add a track or project, or open a screen — it proposes the change and you confirm with one tap before anything is written. It won't delete anything.

After an artist finishes Origin for the first time, the cinematic handoff opens into an optional one-minute **workspace tour**. It introduces Today, its quick actions, the main navigation, global search, and the floating assistant with spotlighted guidance that uses the same signal language and Spectra light as Origin. The introduction can be skipped immediately, every tour step keeps a Skip action available, and completion is remembered separately for each artist. It does not return during ordinary use once finished or dismissed; choosing **Replay introduction** in Settings deliberately replays both the film and tour. Desktop and phone each spotlight the navigation that is actually visible on that screen.

The tour waits until the Origin arrival animation has fully cleared the screen before it appears, leaving the handoff uninterrupted.

After the tour finishes or is skipped, a removable **Getting started** checklist opens at the side of the workspace. Artists get the music-first path: review the artist profile, add a track, upload a tune, shape the board, review statistics, and connect Spotify. Pros get a separate professional path with no artist-profile, track-upload, or Spotify chores: review their professional profile and artist relationships, set one real follow-up and date, open the right working view, and choose notification signal. Its wording adapts from the roles they chose in Passage—distinct starting points for management, labels/A&R, collectives, publicity/marketing, touring/booking, creative work, production, publishing, and assistants—with a general professional fallback for custom or mixed work. Passage roles personalize this checklist only; they never grant access. Progress follows the account, the panel can be collapsed, and a removed checklist can be restored from the Beta page. The first visit to Calendar, Board, Tracks, Projects, Tasks, Artist, Social, Scenes, Stats, or Settings can also open one short contextual introduction. Artists make that choice through the post-Origin workspace tour. After Passage, Pros instead get one plain choice: **Show me the Pro guides** or **Skip all tours**. That decision follows the Pro account across logins and devices, remains separate from any artist tour on a dual account, and is never asked again. Pros never receive the artist catalog tour, and no individual Pro page guide opens unless they opt in. Clicking the version in the rail opens **TEMPO Beta**, which explains the current build, beta expectations, privacy, known platform limits, and how to message Nikita.

**Today.** A greeting banner shows the date, and — when you've uploaded one — your artist logo large in the bottom-right. In a **music-focus** space it also shows three quick counts (active tracks, tasks due this week, sessions this week) and a plain-English line on how much focus time you've logged this week; below that, **Tasks due** (checkable, overdue in warm red) and **Needs attention** — active tracks ordered by transparent reasons (blocked, waiting, overdue next move, quiet too long, and so on), each with a short explanation and a one-tap action like Open or Set next move (four at a time until you show more); quick actions are + Track, + Task, Log session, Start focus; and a **cover art strip** at the bottom drifts your tracks in two slow opposite rows. Hovering slows a row substantially and reveals a black-gradient title and artist strip: the main artwork plays or pauses the current bounce, while the title strip opens the track page. Those controls remain visible on touch screens. Tracks without uploaded art get a temporary Spectra cover shaped by the track's title — vertical light slits from a design language (ice / white / amber / gray), over a soft atmospheric background with a broad hush of colors so covers are easier to tell apart. That same placeholder shows anywhere artwork would (Today, Tracks, Board, track header, projects). In a **tasks-focus** space, Today instead shows Due / Open / Projects counts, quick actions are just + Task and + Project, and the lower section lists your open tasks alongside project progress — no tracks, sessions, or cover strip, since the space doesn't use them. If a focus session is running anywhere, a banner up top lets you jump back in (or close it out if it's been running unusually long). An empty Today uses a contained light panel with a short invite — to bring your existing catalog in for a music space, or to add a task for a tasks-focus space.

Below the hero, a music-focus space also shows **Your TEMPO loop** (renamed **Keep the loop moving** once you've made progress): one current suggestion drawn from what's actually in your catalog — bring in a song, name its next move, work a focused session, upload a bounce, get another pair of ears on it, or close the loop once feedback comes back — with a one-sentence reason and a single button that opens the right screen. A quiet progress line lists what you've already done; nothing is graded or scored. "Not now" hides it for a week, and "Hide this guide" turns it off until you switch it back on from Settings. Existing artists see it collapsed to a one-line invitation rather than the full guide. Once the loop is complete it shows once more and then stays out of the way. Just below it, a small **Pulse** card can summarize a couple of things that changed while you were away — it never repeats anything already shown in Tasks due or Needs attention, and disappears entirely once you're caught up.

**Origin — the first time you arrive.** A brand-new artist doesn't land in an empty workspace. Origin opens on a faint signal that the artist tunes into, names, and gives a history through the moments, influences, and instincts that shaped the work. It then asks what they are making right now and what they want it to feel like, then offers an optional look — color scheme, logo, profile image, and banner — so Today and Artist already feel like theirs before the story scroll. The same step asks whether they want to be on the member network or keep the workspace to themselves; private is preselected, and choosing to join asks for a handle there and then. Nothing in that look step is required; **Skip for now** or Continue with Spectra both move on and stay private, and every choice can be changed later in Settings. A quiet Tempo Theme bed starts with the **Tune in** gesture and loops softly through the experience. The artist can speak or type; both paths are equal and every answer stays theirs to edit. On TEMPO Desktop, spoken words appear in the field as each phrase is heard, not only after Stop, and the bottom-left zoom control (plus Ctrl/Cmd +/-) scales the onboarding text if it feels small. There is no separate recap screen. The scrolling reveal is the single place where the signal reflects the artist's words back as **About the artist**, **The sound**, **Right now**, and a modular **The story**, followed by **Bring the work into range** and **Keep tuning**. Each question panel stays on the film; if there's more than fits, it scrolls inside the glass so Continue is always in reach. Long story chapters sit in a wider middle-left glass panel and scroll inside it so Edit stays on screen. Matching Spotify releases during import does the same — the catalog list scrolls in place rather than running off the screen. Every chapter has a small Back control, and going backward preserves the draft. The reading never declares a destiny, looks the artist up, invents releases or collaborators, or guesses at anything personal. About identifies who the artist is and what music they make; The sound describes audible qualities rather than repeating genres or location; Right now stays in the present; and story chapters carry the history without duplicating the bio. Every part is editable, and story chapters can be added, removed, and reordered. Bringing existing music in remains inside the film, and nothing reaches the catalog until the artist approves the final plan. Nothing is published or shared automatically. Approved details fill only still-empty profile fields and never change profile visibility. The name TEMPO appears only at the final threshold into the workspace, where the soundtrack fades with the closing film. Existing artists are never forced into Origin; it remains available under Settings → Artist Origin to revisit or replay, and it can be skipped without losing what was already entered.

**Passage, arriving as a Pro.** People who aren't artists are **Pros**, not "team members": they hold too many different roles for one label. Anyone joining as a Pro runs Passage instead of Origin: the same film, the same scrolling story and Tempo Theme bed, with questions written for the people around the music rather than the person making it. A fresh Passage starts its theme with **Tune in**; after that gesture, a quiet Sign out control appears on the left, and TEMPO Desktop also offers the same bottom-left onboarding zoom controls as Origin. A saved Passage resumes the theme on the first browser interaction or immediately in TEMPO Desktop, so returning to a question never leaves the film silent. The profile photo chosen there becomes the same personal photo shown on the Pro profile page, in the account menu, and to the artist teams they work with. Both routes in are identical. An admin inviting them through Admin → Invites and an artist adding them from Team land on the same screens with the same detail. Track collaborators, added to comment on a single song, are not Pros and never see it.

Creating the account itself does not ask for a name: onboarding does, so asking at sign-up meant asking twice within a minute. Passage collects it and publishes it, so artists they work with and people on Social see a person rather than a placeholder. If someone joins the network during Origin or Passage, that confirmed name reaches their network identity before any automatic follows are created, so follow notifications identify the artist or Pro rather than showing part of an email address. Its confirmed headline, introduction, roles, day-to-day work, and career chapters fill the corresponding still-empty professional profile fields before the Pro enters the workspace; direct answers stand in if the closing writing is empty, and later edits are never overwritten. Its look step also offers the same private-by-default network choice and live handle check as Origin, so a Pro can claim their @name before entering the workspace or do it later from Profile.

It never asks for an artist name, never describes them as the signal, and never asks them to bring music in. It opens on their name, then asks what describes them, and they can **pick as many as apply**: manager, label owner, collective founder, A&R, booking agent, publicist, tour manager, creative director, visual artist, photographer, engineer, marketing, publisher, assistant, or their own words. Most people wear more than one hat. Then it asks what got them into the music industry, who or what they support, and what they actually do day to day, and they set colors, logo, profile image and banner for their own workspace exactly as an artist does. Question panels stay on the film; if there's more than fits, they scroll inside the glass so Continue is always in reach. They can speak or type every open answer; both paths are equal, and a blocked microphone costs nothing but the dictation.

The closing scroll is **written for them, not read back to them**. TEMPO reads the answers together, finds the thread running through them, and returns a headline, a short introduction, and a few story sections as one connected piece. Every word of it is editable before it is kept. The writing is grounded in what they actually said: it invents no roster names, credits, employers or numbers, never implies they make the music, and says less when they gave less. If the writing fails or they answered nothing, their own answers stand in, so the ending is never blank. Nothing collected grants access or changes what they can do.

Nothing in it is required. Every question can be passed, and **Skip for now** leaves the whole flow from any step without losing what was already written; it stays in Settings. Someone who has already been through it, or who is being onboarded as an artist, is never sent back into it: an unfinished Origin always wins.

A Pro's workspace is not an artist's. They get Today, Calendar, a professional workflow Board, Projects, Tasks, Profile, **Artists**, and Social, with no Tracks or artist identity editor. Their Board moves professional tasks through To do, In progress, and Done rather than treating songs like a production pipeline.

Once inside, a Pro gets their own **guided page tour**, in the same style as the artist one and written from their side of the work: a manager opening Calendar is looking at other people's dates, not their own release. Every page in their rail has one, they appear once each, and taking or skipping one never affects the artist set for a dual account.

Which arrival someone gets is decided in one place, and the desktop app follows the same rule rather than carrying its own. Origin is only ever offered to an account that actually owns an unfinished music artist, so a Pro whose workspace has not been created yet is never mistaken for a new musician.

An unfinished first-time Origin is a hard gate in front of the workspace rather
than a page the artist can accidentally leave behind. Draft copy and its stable
section are saved to the artist's private Origin record, with an immediate
same-browser safety copy covering the short cloud-autosave window. Signing in
again or returning directly to a workspace URL resumes that section before the
dashboard shell is allowed to render. The gate opens only after Origin is
completed, deliberately skipped, or belongs to an artist that predates Origin.

Origin's first scrolling chapter carries a prominent animated **Scroll to reveal your story** prompt, changes to **Swipe up** on a phone, names the six chapters ahead, and can be tapped to move forward. The gesture is explicit even before the artist tries to scroll.

**Bringing your music in.** You don't have to type your catalog in by hand. For a new account, **Bring your music in** is a set of chapters inside Origin; it also stays available as its own screen afterwards under Settings → Import (and from a link in the New track box, for when adding them one at a time isn't the right tool). It works as a conversation: type in the message box, hit the mic and talk (your words appear live as you speak, so you can fix a misheard title before sending), or drop files straight into the thread — screenshots of project folders, a notes app, a whiteboard, a distributor page, or a spreadsheet, PDF, Word file, or plain text. Drop a TEMPO catalog export from Settings and it offers a direct merge restore instead of guessing from scratch. Everything else goes into one thread; you don't have to choose the "right" format first. TEMPO reads each thing as it arrives and answers — it says what it actually found ("that screenshot lists 12 folders; looks like 8 songs") and asks a couple of basics, then stops digging so you aren't stuck in endless follow-ups. Once the basics are in, it tells you to hit **That's everything** to build the workspace, or keep adding detail only if you want it tuned further. You can answer in your own words or tap one of the suggested answers, and remove anything you'd rather not send. It states plainly that what you add is sent off to be read.

TEMPO then reads it all together and proposes a workspace: tracks with a type, stage and momentum, EPs and albums, tasks, target dates, BPMs and keys, next moves, and who you're waiting on. **Nothing is added to your catalog yet.** You get a review screen split into "Ready to add" and "Needs your eyes", where every value is editable and each one tells you where it came from and how confident TEMPO is — "From the folder 'EP Echoes'", "The spreadsheet lists this as out in March". Where something is genuinely ambiguous — three files that look like the same song at different stages, a title you already have, four tracks under one folder name that might be an EP — it asks you instead of guessing, with one-tap answers you can also type over, and redrafts using what you told it. Untick anything you don't want. A final screen shows exactly what will be created, and only then does "Build my TEMPO workspace" write anything — all at once, so a failure leaves you with nothing rather than half a catalog, and pressing it twice can't duplicate your catalog. Discarding an import deletes everything it held, including the files you uploaded, and those files are deleted once the workspace is built too. TEMPO won't search the internet for your discography, guess songwriting credits, or invent a release plan — it organises what you actually gave it. Song titles keep featuring credits exactly as they showed up (like "Midnight (feat. Lena)"); names it spots as collaborators are also kept as a note on the track, but inviting people properly still happens from the track itself. Audio bounces aren't part of this yet — this brings in the shape of your catalog, and you upload bounces to tracks as usual.

**Exploring a demo instead.** Handing over a catalog is a large first ask, so wherever TEMPO offers to bring your music in — the Import chapter inside Origin, and the Import screen afterwards — it also offers to load a demo artist. The demo is a sample catalog built around PRESIDENT, an anonymous masked band four weeks out from their debut album: released singles and unreleased album tracks sitting at different stages on the board, the album and the EP as projects, launch tasks with real deadlines, several weeks of logged focus sessions, guest feedback still open on a few tracks, sticky notes where the next record is being sketched, and a fully written artist page. It fills Today, Board, Tracks, Projects, Tasks, Calendar, Stats and Artist so every screen can be seen holding a real record in progress.

The demo arrives as a whole additional artist beside your own rather than as rows mixed into your catalog. That means it can never be confused with your work, and removing it is one action. Choosing **Explore demo** opens it immediately through a server-confirmed handoff and leaves it available in the workspace switcher; if creation was interrupted, TEMPO recognizes the partial sample and rebuilds it instead of reopening an empty shell. A Pro's next login starts in their own personal Home rather than silently resuming sample data. While it's the artist you're viewing, a strip across the top of every screen says plainly that the catalog is sample data and offers **Remove demo data**, which deletes the demo artist and everything belonging to it — tracks, projects, tasks, sessions, events, feedback and its artist page. Your own artist, its catalog and its profile are never touched by any of it, so if you started exploring partway through setting your own artist up, removing the demo puts you back exactly where you left off with nothing lost. The demo's profile portrait and live banner are bundled once with TEMPO, and its Spotify-linked songs use the catalog's shared cover URLs, so every visitor sees a finished visual workspace without a duplicate set of images being copied into their private storage. The released songs play through Spotify, while the unreleased album tracks and early-stage sketches stay silent, which is what they would be in real life; uploading a bounce is still what gives a track TEMPO's own waveform, timestamped comments and version history. Calendar includes a clearly invented run of demo shows and production holds, Campaign includes separate social, live-production and merch projects, and Social uses a labelled read-only preview with recognisable rock artists rather than borrowing arbitrary member or test profiles. The demo offers the workspace tour independently; on removal, repeating tours on the real artist is optional and off by default.

Titles, release dates, track order and running times come from PRESIDENT's published discography, read from their live catalog rather than transcribed. Details TEMPO stores that no public record holds — BPMs, keys, session notes, feedback — are invented so those features have something to display. So are six early-stage songs grouped under a project named "Record two (working)": a released discography has nothing sitting in Idea, Writing or Production, so without them the first half of the board would always be empty, and that is the half a new artist most needs to see working. They are never presented as real releases. The demo's artist profile stays private and is never published to the member network or a public link.

**Artists.** One account can hold more than one artist name. Every space, and so every track, project and task, belongs to an artist; you switch between them from the left rail, and manage them (add, rename, reorder, delete) in Settings. An existing catalog is moved onto a single artist automatically, so if you only ever release under one name you never have to think about this. Deleting an artist is a hard delete and tells you first exactly how many spaces and tracks go with it; you always keep at least one.

Each artist carries its own look. You pick an accent palette from a fixed set of color options (including a black-and-white Noir), or open **Custom** for your own Cool / Warm colors, upload a **logo** (the wide mark that shows large on Today) and an optional **profile image** (either a portrait or an emblem), and set a banner — your own image, a deep color wash, or a two-color gradient via a Custom button. Profile images appear as circular avatars in the feed, messages, and around the Social globe; in profile headers they sit larger beside the artist name as a complete circle, blending into the banner through a broad artist-colour halo. A profile with no uploaded image leaves the header clean, while compact surfaces use a readable palette-coloured monogram set into a dark signal disc with subtle orbit accents. TEMPO's assistant keeps TEMPO's compact equalizer mark rather than borrowing the artist's identity. The palette follows that artist everywhere the app uses color to mean something: buttons and links, the board's stage-progress colours, the placeholder covers TEMPO draws for tracks with no artwork (so a catalog of un-arted tracks reads as one set), the waveform player, and the moving light field that shows through Today and the edge strips. A bounce shared through a guest review link shows that artist's colours to whoever opens it. The banner appears behind the greeting on Today and on the artist's card in Settings; the logo shows bottom-right on Today (and in that Settings banner preview).

The customization is deliberately bounded so TEMPO still looks like TEMPO for every artist: the palettes are curated pairs rather than a free colour picker, backgrounds and text stay fixed, green still means done and red still means blocked no matter whose workspace you're in, and the TEMPO wordmark in the rail is never replaced by an artist's logo. An artist with nothing customized looks exactly like TEMPO always has.

**Spaces.** On first sign-in TEMPO creates two workspaces for your first artist: **Originals** and **Edits & Remixes**, each with a default stage pipeline. A new artist gets the same two to start. A Pro begins with a private **Home** Space and can add as many professional Spaces as they need—for example Management, Campaigns, Touring, or a separate room for one client. Switch Spaces from the rail; manage them (create, rename, reorder, and delete) in Settings. Artist Spaces belong to the artist currently selected. Pro Spaces belong only to the Pro home, scope its projects, tasks, and calendar work, and never grant or reveal access to an artist workspace. First sign-in also seeds four checklist templates.

Every artist Space has a **focus**, set when you create it and changeable anytime in Settings: **Music** (the default — Board and a stage pipeline for tracks) or **Tasks & projects** (no song board — for non-music work like running social media, where Tasks and Projects are the whole point). Pro Spaces are always **Tasks & projects** focused and each one has a separate workflow Board for its tasks: cards move through To do, In progress, and Done, and every move stays in sync with the Tasks page. The Pro Board never exposes artist Tracks, notes, or stage pipelines. Switching an artist Space's focus doesn't move or delete anything; it only changes what the rail and Today show for that Space. Tasks and projects belong to whichever Space was active when you created them, and only show up in that Space.

**Artist profile.** **Artist** in the rail is a public-ready portrait of the work rather than a stack of biography fields. The banner, profile image, logo, name and tagline lead, followed by up to six deliberately featured music links. **About** explains who the artist is and what music they make, with genres and roles integrated into the same section. **Right now** shows the release, direction, or question currently carrying energy. **The sound** holds up to five audible qualities, contrasts, or ideas in the work. **Listen and connect** gives outside links their own compact home. **The story** is a set of artist-owned chapters displayed openly on the page; each chapter can be added, edited, removed, and reordered from Origin or the profile editor. Origin can provide the first draft of each public-facing section while every part remains editable. Private creative guidance remains in Origin and is not shown on the public profile. The editor also holds location, pronouns, a handle, up to 12 general links, and who can message you. Location suggests cities as you type and places the artist on the Social globe. Sharing stays compact in the header: profiles remain **Private** by default, or can be made visible to **TEMPO members** or at a **Public** link. Joining the network at either level asks for a handle first, wherever you join from — onboarding, Social, or here — and checks whether it is free as you type. A Preview action shows what others will see. The handle is the short lowercase @name used for that address and @mentions; the artist name remains prominent everywhere else. Signed-in profiles retain Follow and Message actions.

**Pro profile.** **Profile** in a Pro home is the person's full professional identity, not a reduced artist card. Name and photo stay consistent anywhere they appear on a team or the network. The editable profile adds a unique TEMPO handle, professional headline, introduction, roles, location, pronouns, current focus, outside links, messaging preference, and a chapter-based **Career story** for meaningful milestones and changes in direction. Passage roles and its closing professional story become an editable starting draft when those fields are still empty; nothing is silently published. A Pro chooses **Private**, **TEMPO members**, or **Public** visibility with the same handle availability rules as artists. In-app and public handle pages label the identity as a Pro profile, use career language, and omit artist-only genres, sound markers, song pipelines, Tracks, and released music.

**Social.** **Social** in the rail is your network layer, and everything on it unlocks once you're on the network — when you’re off it, one clear **Join as TEMPO member** action sits in the middle of the page and asks you to pick your handle before it publishes anything (you can also change that on Artist, or in Settings → Look if you’re on a team rather than as an artist). A slowly spinning **globe** sits under the tabs at all times — cresting up from the page (about two-thirds of the sphere) and dissolving softly into the wash, without a hard rectangular frame — pinning you and the people you're connected to where you actually are in the world, drawn from the location on each artist's profile (anyone who hasn't set one simply isn't plotted). Scroll to zoom in; when several people sit close together (a busy Europe, for example), only some faces show until you zoom closer and they have room to separate. There's no hard ring around the planet; just a soft atmosphere past the edge. Hovering a face eases the spin down and shows their name, how you know them, and where they are; clicking opens their profile. You can drag the globe to spin it yourself. After its first quiet lap it also tips a little on its axis so places like Australia and southern South America drift into view, then eases back. It's painted in your own artist palette, with the color washing slowly across the continents. Three tabs sit above it. **Top 8** is your own pick of up to eight people pinned for quick access — chosen from anyone you follow or any contact linked to a TEMPO profile — each one clicking through to their page. **Follows** shows who you follow and who follows you. **Discover** searches published artist profiles by name, and before you type anything it lists artists you've recently worked with, so it's a place to reconnect rather than an empty search box. You can also **invite an artist friend** who isn’t on TEMPO yet — that request waits for program approval during beta, the same as asking from a track’s People tab. Down the right-hand side, a **feed** column lets you post updates (optional image, optional attach one of your own tracks), @mention handles, like and comment; the composer starts as a single line and opens up when you click into it, visibility is followers-only, TEMPO members, or public, and your home feed is your posts plus posts from people you follow. A post can be reported from the feed, and another artist can be reported from their published profile; the operator receives only that specific public item. **Messages** (from Social or its own screen) is direct messaging between artist profiles, with unread counts that clear when you open the thread.

**Scenes.** A Scene is a complete network for people making or learning
together: a music collective, label roster, school cohort, local community,
writing group, critique circle, or another kind of gathering. It can live
inside an artist's TEMPO workspace or run independently from the dedicated
Scenes home, so joining one never requires creating an artist or exposing a
catalog. Scene banners, scene photos, and artist profile images keep a stable link for the session after the first open (so revisiting does not re-fetch every picture). Scene cards on the list show the banner with a frosted glass strip
for the name, tagline, and member count. Each member has a Scene identity with their own name, picture, bio,
location, and links; it can optionally connect to an artist profile without
being owned by it.

Every Scene opens on **Pulse**, a visual dashboard combining the current
conversation with recently active people, recognition, and useful signals.
Owners arrange the network from configurable sections: discussion boards
with posts, questions, polls, announcements, pins, comments, and mentions;
multiple live chat rooms; event calendars with capacity-aware RSVPs;
libraries for replays, guides, files, links, and templates; member showcases
for work and feedback requests; and composed pages for welcome material,
rules, programs, or other evergreen information. Private groups can give a
cohort, host team, editors' room, or other circle access to only the sections
meant for them. Being in a Scene grants no access to anyone's tracks,
projects, notes, or other private TEMPO work.

**Scene Studio** is the independent management surface. It shows network
health, lets owners and moderators add and organize sections and groups,
shape the member experience, and tune the Scene's appearance. Uploaded
banners keep a generous responsive canvas and an editable focal point;
palette light and texture stay present over the artwork, while the emblem
and Scene identity sit in a deliberate information block rather than between
unrelated rows. Listed Scenes can present selected pages, events, resources,
and showcase work on a public front door while member discussions and chats
remain private. Owners can also install a complete **Owl's Nest** demo with
sample members, posts, chat, an event, resources, a welcome page, showcase
work, and recognition to see the system populated before building their own.
That installer is available directly from Scene Studio even when the account
does not yet manage a Scene.

Scenes still support open, approval-required, and invite-only doors;
ownership and moderator roles; requests, invitations, removal, bans, and
auditable moderation. Joining is attached to the signed-in account's Scene
identity (optionally linked to its active artist profile), so the same Scene
cannot acquire conflicting memberships when someone switches artists or uses
another account. Database migrations 049 through 067 are required for the
complete network experience.

**Stats.** Today, Board, Tracks and Tasks all show one space at a time; **Stats** (formerly "Artist", renamed and moved to its own tab when the Artist tab became the public profile page above) shows the whole artist's numbers at once, across every space they own. Reach it from the rail or the artist dropdown. It opens on that artist's banner and logo with a trimmed headline row — tracks, released, focus time — then leads with **artist attributes** (see below) as the page's one feature section, followed by **the year in bounces** (twelve months of bounces uploaded against tracks started, so a year of work reads at a glance) and the rest: **pipeline** (where tracks sit in each space's stages, one bar per space, with spaces that have nothing staged collected into a single quiet line); a grouped **Spotify / SoundCloud / Apple Music** card, tabbed rather than three stacked panels; **momentum**; **work rhythm** (when you actually work, by day and hour, with your busiest day, busiest hour and how many weeks in a row you've kept going); **releases** (countdowns to what's coming and a strip of what's already out); **spaces** (every space of that artist side by side with its own counts and when it was last touched — tap one to switch to it); **your sound** (the shape of your tempos, the keys you write in most, your genres and track types); **longest in progress** (the oldest unfinished tracks and how long each has sat in its current stage); and a compact **Signals** card combining listening time, sessions logged, spaces, and feedback received (from guests versus your own notes, what's still open, and approvals) into one small panel instead of two. Every number comes from what's actually in your TEMPO — nothing is estimated or guessed. Like the rest of the modular layout, any section can still be dragged, tabbed together, or hidden — this is just the starting arrangement.

**Streaming platforms.** The Stats page can also show how your music is doing outside TEMPO. Paste your profile link once per platform. **SoundCloud** is the one that gives real numbers — plays, followers, likes and reposts, plus your most-played tracks. **Spotify** and **Apple Music** list your releases as each service has them, and nothing more: Spotify removed follower counts, popularity and top tracks from its API in February 2026, and Apple's statistics need a paid developer membership. Every section states plainly what it cannot show. No platform has ever published real stream counts or monthly listeners to outside apps — Spotify's own live only in Spotify for Artists — and TEMPO never estimates or fills in the gaps.

Because SoundCloud only ever hands over *today's* totals and no history, TEMPO writes the numbers down once a day and draws the trend itself. A freshly linked section shows a flat figure on the first day and a line from the second onwards, so the curve builds the longer you use it. Linking is per artist, so each artist name you release under points at its own profiles. You never need a developer account of your own: the platform keys belong to TEMPO and are shared by everyone using it, so an artist's whole setup is pasting a link.

**Artist attributes.** Stats also leads with a personal, six-figure read on how you actually work: **Output** (how much you finish), **Velocity** (how fast work moves once it starts), **Follow-Through** (how much of what you start you finish), **Consistency** (how regularly you show up), **Stage Presence** (how much you play live), and **Reach** (audience you can measure on linked platforms). It opens compact by default — six figures, no radar — since a first look shouldn't feel like opening a character sheet; switch to Full for the hexagon and a deeper per-figure breakdown. Every figure is built from real actions — bounces uploaded, tracks reaching a final stage, forward moves on the board, focus sessions, shows logged, platform follower counts — never a guess or a hidden formula. Tapping any figure opens exactly what earned it: a list of the real events behind it and the published scale that turned the total into a number, so nothing here is handed down as a verdict. A signal that can't honestly be measured yet (no shows logged, no platform linked, stage timing still warming up) shows as unmeasured rather than a low score. Real work also earns achievements — around a hundred of them, from your first bounce to a full year of consistent activity — each graded Glimmer through Corona along the same ice-to-amber flare used throughout TEMPO, with a handful kept unnamed until you earn them. Achievements opens on every one you've unlocked so you can scroll that list; "Show full catalog" reveals the rest, grouped by grade, with earned ones lit on the ice-to-amber flare and locked ones dashed and dim. A pop-up marks the moment an achievement unlocks. Existing history (finished tracks, focus sessions, Origin, the getting-started checklist) is credited retroactively the first time you open the sheet. A **Live** section lets you log shows and festivals played, including pulling in past calendar entries you tick off yourself — nothing is imported automatically. Attributes and achievements are personal and live in one place: they never appear on your public artist page or the Artist page, and can be dimmed to a plain list of figures or hidden entirely, the same as any other Stats section.

The page is **modular**, the same way a track workspace is. "Edit layout" in the top-right of the banner lets you drag sections between the two columns, reorder them, drop one onto another to combine them into a tabbed panel, and hide anything you don't want — hidden sections stay one click away in a tray, and Reset puts everything back. From the same panel you can start from a template — **Overview** (everything), **Minimal**, **Statistics**, or **Platforms** — each a different focused starting arrangement you can still rearrange freely afterwards. Your arrangement is remembered per artist, on your account — the same however you sign in. The charts use that artist's own Cool / Warm colours, adjusted so large filled shapes stay readable against the dark background, and stage colours match the Board's cold-to-hot ramp so the two screens agree.

**Custom stat modules.** Anything worth tracking that TEMPO doesn't already show — sync placements, merch sold, radio spins, an Instagram follower count, whatever — gets its own module you build yourself. From "Edit layout", add a new custom module and give it a name; it lands on the page like any other section. Inside it, add as many named stats as you want (an optional unit like "$" or "plays"), and log a reading for any date right from the card — no need to re-open Edit layout. Log as often as you actually update the number; each reading is kept, so a stat with two or more readings draws its own filled area chart with a date underneath each end. Made a mistake? Each stat shows its last few readings with a one-tap undo. Rename or remove a whole module from its own header. These numbers are the one thing on the Artist page TEMPO doesn't calculate itself — you're the source of truth for them.

**Board.** Kanban for the active space: drag tracks and sticky notes between stages, Sort and Filter from compact header menus (type, tag, attention; order cards in a column by Custom, title, updated, or deadline), choose Comfortable or Compact card density, tap a track card to open the workspace. Each stage has a **+** to place an existing track that has no stage, start a new track there, or add a sticky note (title + optional text — board-only, not in Tracks). Tracks with no stage stay hidden on the Board until you add them from that **+** menu (they’re still in Tracks). A muted **Remove** on the card clears the stage without deleting the track. Comfortable cards show cover, type, and next move; Compact is a thin text row (title plus a tiny momentum mark) so dense boards stay scannable. The whole pipeline stays on screen without scrolling sideways — stages share the available width, and a stage holding nothing shrinks to a slim labelled strip until you start dragging, at which point every stage opens up so you can drop anywhere. On a phone or a narrow window the stages stack vertically instead. Cards show a small mark when something’s blocked or overdue.

**Tracks.** Create with title and type (more details optional). The Tracks page lists everything in the active space — Sort and Filter open from compact header menus (Custom, title, stage, updated, deadline, plus any saved orders; type, stage, tag, attention), and Comfortable / Compact density matches the Board (Compact is a thin text row). Custom is the default: drag the handle to rearrange. You can also split the list into **groups** (an album, EP, playlist, or any bucket you name) — separate from Projects. Create a group from the header, drag tracks into it, or remove a group without deleting its tracks (they simply stop being in one). Each group can carry an optional cover image (shown as a thumbnail beside the name) and an optional color that tints the whole block — one of five fixed swatches or any custom color you pick — so an EP reads as one thing rather than another anonymous list; a group with neither looks plain. Reorder the groups themselves by dragging the handle beside the name, or with the arrows. Tracks that aren't in any group get no heading at all and sit at the top of the list by default; you can move a group above them if you'd rather. Group covers and colors need migration 044; a custom (rather than preset) color needs migration 045. **Save** names the current arrangement and adds it to Sort so you can return to it later (apply it as Custom, overwrite it, or remove it). Hit "Select" to tick several tracks and act on them together — move them into a group (or ungroup), start a new group from the selection, move them to a stage, add them to a project (or remove them from one), or delete them in one go with a confirmation naming each one.

**Track workspace.** A studio-feeling page built around listening. The header shows artwork (or a gradient placeholder, tap to set cover) tinted with a soft wash of the artwork's own color, editable title, mono meta line, momentum, and deadline. Compact **Previous** and **Next** controls follow the current space's track order, so you can move through the catalog without returning to Board or Tracks. Below it, a stage timeline shows every stage as a row you click through (or move between with arrow keys) — jumping more than one stage at once asks you to confirm, and a small lightning-bolt mark shows which stages have an automation ("recipe") set up. If a stage's recipe is in "preview" mode, moving into it opens a dialog listing what it would do so you can pick which parts to apply; "automatic" mode runs everything right away with a toast you can undo. The main column is the waveform player (current or selected version, A/B via dropdown, with small markers on the waveform wherever a comment is pinned and an "Add comment here" button next to play/pause), a version timeline (upload with “what changed?”, set current, download, delete, pin as a milestone, record a decision — see below — every version stays in the track's history for good, while the cloud itself keeps only the current bounce and the one before it; wav/aiff are converted to mp3 in the browser before upload; deleting a version that's pinned, has comments/decisions, or is on an active guest link tells you so before you confirm), a guest review links section for sharing a bounce outside TEMPO, a Now / Next / Blocked / Target strip for tracking your next move, who you're waiting on, any blocker, and your target date (with a short list of plain-English attention flags underneath, including how many comments are still open), a workflow strip for starting/continuing a focus session and seeing recent stage automations (with a retry for anything that failed), and the session log. **The whole page is modular.** Every section — player, versions, workflow strip, guest links, session log, checklist, comments, files, notes, references, people, activity and details (metadata plus Delete track) — is a module you can arrange. "Edit layout" lets you drag modules between the left and right columns, reorder them, and hide what you don't need; dropping one module onto another combines the two into a tabbed panel so related tools share a single space. A divider between the columns can be dragged (or nudged with arrow keys) to set how wide each side is. Presets — Writing, Production, Feedback, Mix review, Release prep — each lay out a focused arrangement for that kind of session rather than showing everything at once, and anything a preset leaves out stays one click away in a "Hidden" tray. The waveform can't be hidden while a track has bounces. Layouts save per track and are personal to you — collaborators keep their own. An **Edit** menu in the bottom-right of the track's header holds "Edit layout" and "Delete track", so both stay reachable whatever modules your layout is showing. Deleting asks you to confirm and tells you its bounces, comments, checklists and session history go with it. Uploading artwork (from the header or Files tab) sets the track cover. Audio lives in private cloud storage and plays via time-limited links; large bounces need your Storage size limit raised in Supabase if uploads over ~50 MB fail.

**Milestones, decisions & blind A/B.** Pin any version as a milestone (rough mix, final master, etc. — with an optional label) so it stands out in the version timeline; unpinning warns you first if that bounce would then become eligible for automatic cleanup. Record a decision on a version — approved, needs changes, or rejected, with an area (mix, master, arrangement, artwork, general) and a note — decisions pile up as an append-only log, with the latest shown right on the version row. To compare two versions blind, select them in the timeline and start a Blind A/B: which one is "A" and which is "B" is shuffled and hidden, you play one at a time at your own volume for each (shared scrub stays put when you hit play, including on TEMPO Desktop), and only once you reveal do you see which was which — at that point you can log your pick and optionally record it as a decision.

**Stage recipes.** Any stage can have a recipe — one or more automations that fire when a track moves into it: apply a checklist template, create a task, set the track's next move, set momentum, or ask for a decision on the current version. Recipes run in "preview" mode by default (you get to pick which actions to apply each time) or can be switched to "automatic" (they run immediately, with a toast offering to undo the stage change). Writing, Production, Mixdown, Master, and Release Prep stages offer a one-click "add suggested recipe" starting point. This applies everywhere a track's stage changes — dragging on the Board, clicking through the track's own timeline, or using the stage dropdown.

**Focus sessions.** Start a focus session from a track's page or from Today — give it a goal and, optionally, pull in a few checklist items to work from. That drops you into a stripped-down session screen: the track's identity, your goal, a live elapsed timer, the waveform, your chosen checklist items, and private scratch notes (not saved anywhere else — leaving with unsaved notes prompts a confirm). Ending a session asks what changed, what's left, and your next move (with the option to set that as the track's official next move), lets you check off any of the session's checklist items, and lets you bounce up a new version right there. Abandoning a session discards the timer without logging it as complete. Sessions still running after 24 hours get a "close or keep going" prompt instead of a plain continue. The track's session log shows goal, duration, and outcome for these alongside older, simpler logged sessions.

**References.** A References tab on the track page holds anything that informs the track without being part of it: another track or sound (as an audio clip, optionally with a start/end trim), an image, a link, or a plain note — each with an optional "why this matters" line, reorderable by drag. Audio references play through the same one-at-a-time player as everything else, so they never overlap the main waveform. Star a reference to carry it into your next focus session, where it shows up in a small dedicated section.

**Comments.** Pin feedback to the exact moment in a bounce: click "Add comment here" while listening (or a marker on the waveform) to jump straight to the Comments tab with that timestamp ready to go, or write a general note with no timestamp. Comments can be replied to, edited, resolved (and reopened), or deleted with confirm. Switch between "this version" and "all versions" — clicking a comment from another version loads that bounce and seeks to the right spot automatically. The Comments tab shows how many threads are still open, and that count also shows up in the track's attention flags.

**Guest review links.** Share one specific bounce with someone who doesn't have (or need) a TEMPO account — a mixer, a vocalist, a friend whose ears you trust. From the track page's guest links section, pick a version, an optional label, an expiry (14 days by default, or 7/30/never), and whether they can comment and/or download; TEMPO shows the shareable link exactly once, so copy it before closing. The guest opens a plain, focused page — track title, artwork, that version's waveform, and (if you allowed it) a simple name-and-note comment box, optionally pinned to a moment — with no sign-in, no ability to see your notes, checklist, other versions, or edit/resolve anything, and the page is kept out of search engines. Revoke a link anytime from the same panel without deleting comments the guest already left; expired or revoked links show a calm "not available" page instead of any error that reveals why.

**Tasks layout.** The task list is laid out as four columns — Overdue, Today, This week, Later — so the shape of your week is visible at a glance. Drag a task from one column to another to move its due date: the column you’re over says Move to today / this week / later / overdue. Today becomes today; This week asks which day; Later asks for a date (or none); Overdue asks for a past date. Checked-off work lives in **Closed out** under the columns — a count and a list you can reopen — instead of a faded pile or a button up in the page title. Filters sit in one compact toolbar with a Clear link once anything is set.

**Tasks.** List scoped to the active space, with quick-add that keeps category, status, due date, and assignee visible up front; More details holds optional track and project links plus notes. Categories are shared across the active artist or Pro home: the built-in Social, Outreach, Pitching, Admin, Production, and Other labels can be renamed or recolored, and anyone with Tasks write access can add their own categories. Removing a custom category moves its existing tasks safely to Other. The same names and colors appear on Today, project task lists, Tasks, and the Pro workflow Board. Open work is grouped into Overdue / Today / This week / Later and can be dragged between those buckets; filters by category and status. Closed-out tasks are kept on their own honor list. Linked items show as chips that jump to the track or project.

**Projects.** Card grid scoped to the active space, showing name, type, deadline, track/task counts, and combined checklist %. A project is whatever you call one — create it as General, Single, EP, Album, or Edit pack if that label helps, then attach or detach any number of existing tracks and tasks (or none). It is not a one-track wrapper and not the same thing as a Tracks-page group. Open a project to edit description and deadline. Release-type projects unlock a **release workspace**: release date and countdown, a transparent readiness list (what’s still missing — not a mystery percentage), track order and per-track metadata/credits with copy/CSV export, distribution and pitching fields, a timeline of dates and tasks, an optional “create release plan” preview of suggested tasks, master/artwork warnings when a milestone doesn’t match the current bounce, and a post-release section once a live link and past date exist.

**People & collaboration.** Anyone with a public guest review link can leave a comment on that bounce with just a name — no TEMPO account. On a track’s People tab, the owner can **include a TEMPO artist** already on the network — people they follow, or anyone they find by handle — as a collaborator on that track, or **invite someone by email** (editor, uploader, commenter, or viewer). Email invitees get a simple login for that track only, plus a one-time invite link; they sign in with that email to accept, or create a simplified account from the invite if they don't have one. Included artists are on the track immediately. Collaborators only see that track — not your whole catalog, projects list, or unrelated tasks. During beta, asking TEMPO to also invite an email guest as a **full artist** does not send immediately — it waits for approval on Admin → Invites. Controls they can’t use are hidden or disabled; the database still enforces the rules.

**Team.** Team sits on the left rail under Artist, on its own page. For the artist it opens on a fanned spread of large rectangular photo cards — you front and center, everyone who works with you behind and to the side — then the invite and access tools. You can bring on a manager, agent, tour manager, label contact, or assistant at the artist level instead of one track at a time. Each role starts with a sensible default (an agent gets calendar and performances; a tour manager the same, read-only elsewhere; a label contact sees releases and stats but nothing hands-on) and every grant can be adjusted per person afterwards, area by area, at none / read / write. Invite by **handle or email**. If they’re already on TEMPO, they get a notification and must approve before they join the team — that waiting invite shows on their Team page. If they’re new, TEMPO emails the invitation, with a copyable link as a fallback; they create an account from the invite (no invite code) and skip Origin.

Team access is now explicit across Catalog, Audio & files, Feedback, Tasks, Calendar, Releases, Stats, Performances, Social, and Team. An invitation shows that exact summary before send and again before approval; Stats stays read-only and Social/Team administration stays owner-only. Artists can suspend, resume, or end access with a review of open work, while membership history remains attributable. Tasks can be assigned only to the artist owner or an active eligible teammate, and assignees can safely update status without receiving broader edit permission. The artist Team page separates **People**, an owner-written **Brief**, and work **Waiting** on the team. Each artist can open one private team room inside Messages; members always speak as their own human identity. Enabling the shared team schedule preserves existing calendar ownership and edit timestamps even when its database rollout is retried.

A **Pro** is the person; a **team membership** is that person's relationship to one artist. A Pro signs into **their own account**, not yours, and can hold different roles for different artists. Accepting the invite does not turn them into an artist—or turn an artist they already own into a Pro workspace. One email/login can hold three clearly separate things at once: **My artist**, the person's private **My work** Pro home, and each artist workspace they have been invited into. Their program invitation welcomes them through **Passage** and their professional home rather than asking them to introduce an artist through Origin. Team opens their private operations home with **My Work** across artists, a combined **Schedule**, and their **Roster**; no artist can inspect that combined view or learn about another relationship. Pros can declare optional availability and choose whether its small allowlisted summary is shared. Optional Manager, label, publicist, tour manager, agent, assistant, and custom starter kits preview the private views/templates/examples they would add, combine without duplicates, and never grant artist access or overwrite existing work. On Discover Pros can open anyone’s artist profile without being sent back to their hub. They also have Today, projects, tasks, calendar, multiple private Spaces, and their full professional Profile. Inside an artist workspace a bar shows the relationship and **Back home**; only granted areas appear, and the artist page is read-only. Social people cards show one person with badges — Artist, Manager for {name} — so a dual identity isn’t listed twice. Personal attributes, points, and achievements stay owner-only; a stats grant covers streaming numbers and custom stats, never someone’s own progress.

**Activity & notifications.** Each track has an Activity tab with a readable history of meaningful events (uploads, stage moves, comments, and similar — not every keystroke). The app shell has a notification center with an unread count; you’ll get in-app notices for things like invite acceptance, a team invite waiting on you, and new versions on shared tracks. Program invitations can be delivered by email; there are no general email notifications yet.

**Help & reporting.** **Report a problem** beside Settings opens a short bug, help, or feedback form; the Settings page carries the same control for mobile. The dialog sits over the whole workspace so it stays usable on web and desktop. It sends only what you deliberately type, a privacy-safe label for the current page, and basic browser information. You can also describe a bug or help request to the TEMPO assistant: it prepares the support report and asks you to confirm before sending it.

**Workspace customize.** On a track page, Customize lets you pick a layout preset (Writing, Production, Feedback, Mix review, Release prep, or Custom), reorder or hide optional modules, choose the default work-panel tab, and toggle compact mode. Layouts are yours alone — they don’t change what collaborators see. The waveform stays available whenever a bounce exists.

**Checklists & templates.** Arrangement, Mixdown, Master Prep, and Release Prep ship on first login. Apply a template to a track or save any checklist as a new template.

**Origin motion and sound.** TEMPO’s voice melts from one thought into the next
throughout Origin, not just in its opening lines. Copy and interactive panels
take separate turns rather than overlapping, and each film soundtrack eases
fully to silence before its scene is released. The quiet Tempo Theme beneath
both artist Origin and Pro Passage starts from Tune in, waits for real playback
before fading up, and remains armed through media-readiness and window-focus
races so Mac onboarding does not stay silent after a rejected first attempt.

**Look & feel.** Dark Spectra palette (ice for interaction, amber for “current” / status). Geometric Jura carries the UI — menus, titles, body — with heavier weights on headers and active nav; Inter is used only for numbers so zeros stay clean (Jura’s zero has a little centre dot). Everyday body and meta type sits a touch larger for easier reading. TEMPO's supplied distressed white wordmark is used as artwork across the studio rail and outward-facing entry points rather than recreated with a font; the vertical ice-to-amber light bars remain its compact icon where the wide mark cannot fit. Custom artist logos are images, not another font. Anything you can click that represents a track, project, or task — board cards, list rows, the attention queue on Today — glows softly around the cursor in the app's own ice → white → amber colours (amber-warning automatically if it's blocked or overdue). One gently blurred moving wash now sits behind the full signed-in workspace and the Admin console (it stays put if you enlarge the page on desktop), while the panels above it share a translucent glass treatment with soft top edges and cast shadows. Today, Artist, and Stats heroes keep Spectra behind lighter frost so the lines stay visible, and uploaded banner photos stay translucent enough that the wash still moves through. Existing gradients, artist-colour washes, cursor light, and shader-backed feature areas remain visible through that glass instead of being replaced by a flat style. Today still opens on a hero carrying an oversized greeting, your active / due / session counts as large readable numerals, and the quick actions. The root prismatic light-field remains reserved for the intro, empty states, active navigation marks, and intentional feature moments; the former full-width shader strip at the top is gone. Keyboard focus uses a clear ice ring. Errors surface as short toasts with what to try next.

**Program administration.** TEMPO has a private, unlisted Admin console for running the invited program. From Admin you can return to the regular studio with **Back to TEMPO**, which stays on the left menu while the rest of Admin scrolls. The console shares the studio’s moving wash and glass, and its home screen is a working dashboard rather than a plain list of links. A platform administrator can see account identity, join and sign-in dates, public profile identity, and aggregate usage analytics—including 90-day assistant-request, storage-growth, upload, and focus-time trends, plus an Activation & Pulse section covering signup-to-first-song funnel progress, return rates, and how much the Today guide and Pulse digests get used and opted into — always aggregate counts, never a per-member activity view. Admins can issue or revoke one-person signup links; send a branded email with an individual code that offers **Get the desktop app** and **Open TEMPO in your browser** as two full paths at equal weight, desktop listed first (plus copy-paste addresses if a button fails); see delivery status and resend; see **Invites by others** (team members and collaborators invited from inside TEMPO, named as who invited whom) and **Needs approval** for full-artist invites requested during beta; triage member-submitted bugs, help requests, and product feedback; check operational health (email, schema, events, Pulse delivery); suspend, reactivate, or permanently remove an account; review reports that point to content a member published to the network; and inspect an audit trail of every privileged change. The console cannot fetch members’ track or project names, audio, lyrics, notes, checklists, feedback, ordinary comments, session contents, direct messages, private profiles, or private contact books.

**Pro-to-artist team requests.** The relationship can start from either side without weakening the approval boundary. From **Team → Roster**, a Pro can search published artist profiles, request a management, agent, touring, label, assistant, or custom role, add an introduction, follow its status, and cancel while it waits. The artist reviews those requests in **Team → People** and either declines or chooses the role and exact access to return as an invitation. The request itself never grants access; the Pro still reviews and accepts the invitation before entering the workspace.

**Pro starter kits.** Manager, label, publicist, tour manager, agent, assistant, and custom kits are small private operating systems rather than token examples. Each role contributes two focused working views, four detailed templates or checklists, and two optional example tasks—from release gates and DSP pitches to venue advances, offer comparisons, press outreach, meeting closeout, and weekly operating reviews. Multi-role combinations deduplicate safely; the preview explains every item and exposes the full checklist before installation. A newer kit version only adds new material, so it never overwrites templates, tasks, or examples someone already edited.

## Current interaction refinements

Needs attention on Today shows five items by default and expands on request. Skipping the homepage workspace introduction asks whether to skip only that tour or every remaining contextual tour.

Populated Board stages keep a readable desktop width and form a horizontally scrollable pipeline when they no longer fit; empty stages still collapse and phone layouts still stack. Calendar ships a distinct default color for each workspace date and event type, with a Categories panel for saved label, color, and custom event-category choices through migration 075.

The PRESIDENT demo uses the Blood Of Your Empire artwork as its shared banner, carries a three-paragraph About section, and keeps a moving Social globe with a wider rock and metal circle plus fictional funny and serious conversations. Scenes opens its read-only **It’s Not Just a Phase** emo and old-metal room instead of presenting the demo artist as off the network.

**TEMPO Desktop.** A downloadable Windows and Mac beta uses the same TEMPO workspace in a native app (the download and installer just call it TEMPO). The Windows setup is a short wizard — welcome, choose a folder, optional shortcuts — dressed in TEMPO’s dark look. Mac ships as an unsigned universal build from GitHub Actions — Gatekeeper warns on first open; right-click the app and choose Open. Its local vault mirrors complete bounce history to the computer before older versions leave the cloud, and the timeline shows whether each version is on this computer, in the cloud, or on another computer. Board, Tracks, Projects, Tasks, and Calendar retain cached reads while offline, with supported changes queued until the connection returns, while background sync keeps the catalog ready between sessions. After an invite signup, a welcome screen offers the desktop app and the browser as two full versions of TEMPO before Origin, desktop listed first with what it is better at, and says the account is the same either way; the shared Download page is a public Spectra/glass landing (reachable without signing in), so invite email links land there cleanly. The web rail and Download page offer the installer until a recent desktop device is known, then **Open in desktop** launches the app directly; the desktop rail offers the reciprocal **Open web app** link, which opens that same page in your normal browser rather than another TEMPO window. Only one TEMPO Desktop runs at a time — a second launch (Windows or Mac) focuses the window you already have. The tray and Start icon use the TEMPO emblem. With the window closed to the tray or sitting in the background, a new message raises a larger bottom-right glass toast tinted with your artist Cool/Warm colours, a short preview, and a soft chime. Interface zoom (the on-screen control or Ctrl/Cmd ± / 0) scales the main workspace; when the window is wide enough for the labeled rail, menu type can grow a little with zoom too, while the narrow icon rail stays a stable size.

## How it’s hosted

The live app is at **https://mytempo.dev** (sign-in at `/login`). Pushes to the main branch deploy there automatically. For local work on a Mac, double-click **Launch TEMPO.command** in the project folder — it starts a local server and opens the app in your browser (keep that Terminal window open; Ctrl+C stops it). Local development uses your `.env.local` keys; anything that ships uses the production address for absolute links, not localhost.

The floating assistant’s product reference lives in the repo’s assistant knowledge file — update that when this document’s feature set changes so the assistant stays accurate.
